Proposition 7.1.

Let
\begin{equation*} U=\{x\in\Spa(A,A^\circ):|W(x)|\leq|\varpi(x)|\}. \end{equation*}
Then \(U\) is nonempty, the rational localisation
\begin{equation*} A\longrightarrow \mathcal O(U) =A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \end{equation*}
is not flat, and multiplication by \(Q^2\) is a strict inclusion on \(A\) even though \(Q^2\) maps to zero in \(\mathcal O(U)\text{.}\)

Proof.
The composite
\begin{equation*} A\longrightarrow B=k\langle W,Q\rangle/(Q^2) \longrightarrow k, \qquad f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h\longmapsto f_0(0), \end{equation*}
is norm-nonincreasing. It therefore sends \(A^\circ\) into \(k^\circ\text{,}\) and pulling back the norm valuation on \(k\) gives a continuous valuation belonging to \(\Spa(A,A^\circ)\text{.}\) At this point \(W\) has value zero and \(\varpi\) has nonzero value, so the point lies in \(U\text{.}\)
🔗
Since the restricted Gauss norm on \(A\) is multiplicative and \(\lVert Q\rVert=1\text{,}\) for every \(a\in A\) we have
\begin{equation*} \lVert Q^2a\rVert=\lVert a\rVert. \end{equation*}
Thus multiplication by \(Q^2\) is an isometry. Its map onto its image, with the subspace topology, is consequently a homeomorphism, so the inclusion is strict. Moreover, an isometric image of the complete space \(A\) is closed in \(A\text{.}\) Since \(\lVert Q^2\rVert=1\text{,}\) the element \(Q^2\) is nonzero; multiplication by it is therefore injective because \(A\) is a domain.
🔗
On the other hand, the calculation (4.1) says directly that the canonical map to the completed rational localisation kills \(Q^2\text{.}\) The isomorphism of Proposition 4.1 identifies this localisation with the nonzero ring
\begin{equation*} A\longrightarrow k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X,\quad Q\longmapsto Q. \end{equation*}
If the canonical map were flat, the non-zero-divisor \(Q^2\) would still act injectively on \(\mathcal O(U)\text{;}\) equivalently, tensoring the injection
\begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{\,\cdot Q^2\,}A \end{equation*}
with \(\mathcal O(U)\) would make multiplication by the image of \(Q^2\) injective. Its image is zero, however, so this multiplication map is zero on the nonzero ring \(\mathcal O(U)\text{,}\) a contradiction.
