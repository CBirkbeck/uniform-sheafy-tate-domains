Proposition 8.6. Coefficientwise localisation.

Let \(\alpha\) be a rational datum whose entries lie in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\text{,}\) and put \(P=(\mathcal A^{(N)})_\alpha\text{.}\) There is a bicontinuous ring isomorphism
\begin{equation} \mathcal A_\alpha \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \left\{ \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu: p_\mu\in P,\quad \lVert p_\mu\rVert\longrightarrow0 \right\}.\tag{8.13} \end{equation}
The ring on the right has the supremum norm, and its multiplication is given by (8.11), with \(W\) replaced by its image in \(P\text{.}\) If a rational refinement is also represented by data in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) and \(P\to P'\) is the corresponding restriction map, then the induced restriction sends
\begin{equation} \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu \longmapsto \sum_\mu (p_\mu|_{P'})e_\mu.\tag{8.14} \end{equation}

Proof.
Apply the canonical map \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\to P\) to every coefficient in (8.10). This gives a continuous homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \Phi_0:\mathcal A\longrightarrow \left\{ \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu: p_\mu\in P,\quad \lVert p_\mu\rVert\longrightarrow0 \right\}. \end{equation*}
In the target, the image of \(g\) is a unit and each \(f_i/g\) is of norm at most \(1\text{,}\) by the defining property of \(P\text{.}\) Thus \(\Phi_0\) extends continuously to \(\mathcal A[1/g]\) for the rational-localisation topology, and then to the completion:
\begin{equation*} \Phi:\mathcal A_\alpha\longrightarrow \left\{ \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu: p_\mu\in P,\quad \lVert p_\mu\rVert\longrightarrow0 \right\}. \end{equation*}
🔗
In the other direction, the inclusion \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\to\mathcal A\) gives a continuous homomorphism \(P\to\mathcal A_\alpha\text{.}\) The family \((e_\mu)_\mu\) is bounded in \(\mathcal A_\alpha\text{.}\) Apply the homomorphism to every coefficient. If \(\lVert p_\mu\rVert\to0\text{,}\) the series
\begin{equation*} \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu \end{equation*}
converges in \(\mathcal A_\alpha\text{.}\) The resulting map \(\Psi\) is continuous, and (8.11) shows that it is multiplicative.
🔗
The composite \(\Phi\Psi\) fixes every term \(p e_\mu\text{,}\) and hence every finite sum of such terms. These finite sums are dense. The other composite fixes the dense image of \(\mathcal A[1/g]\text{.}\) Continuity therefore gives \(\Phi\Psi=1\) and \(\Psi\Phi=1\text{,}\) proving (8.13).
🔗
If \(P\to P'\) comes from a rational refinement, all the maps just used commute with this restriction map. On a finite sum, restriction simply applies \(P\to P'\) to each coefficient; continuity gives (8.14) for every convergent sum.
