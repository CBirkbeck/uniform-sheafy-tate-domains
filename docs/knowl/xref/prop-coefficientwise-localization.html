Proposition 7.8. Coefficientwise localisation.

Let \(\alpha\) be a rational datum in \(\mathcal A_N\text{,}\) and put \(P=(\mathcal A_N)_\alpha\text{.}\) There is a bicontinuous ring isomorphism
\begin{equation} \mathcal A_\alpha \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{\,c_0}Pe_\mu.\tag{7.18} \end{equation}
It is natural for rational refinements represented in a finite head: if \(P\to Q\) is the corresponding head restriction map, the induced restriction sends
\begin{equation} \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu \longmapsto \sum_\mu (p_\mu|_Q)e_\mu.\tag{7.19} \end{equation}
The same statement holds after increasing \(N\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let
\begin{equation*} R=\mathcal A_N\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle, \qquad J=\overline{(gT_i-f_i)_i}\subset R, \qquad Q=R/J. \end{equation*}
Evaluation at \(T_i=f_i/g\) identifies \(Q\) with the head rational localisation \(P\text{.}\)
🔗
The tail decomposition applies after adjoining the variables \(T_i\text{.}\) Apply the quotient map \(R\to Q\) to each tail coefficient. Since the rational relations lie in the head, this gives a continuous homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \Phi:\mathcal A_\alpha\longrightarrow \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}Qe_\mu. \end{equation*}
Conversely, the canonical map from \(Q\) to \(\mathcal A_\alpha\) may be applied to every coefficient. For a null family \((q_\mu)_\mu\) the resulting series
\begin{equation*} \sum_\mu q_\mu e_\mu \end{equation*}
converges in \(\mathcal A_\alpha\text{,}\) and defines a continuous homomorphism \(\Psi\) in the other direction. The multiplication rule (7.16) shows that both maps are ring homomorphisms.
🔗
The two composites are the identity on finite tail sums with polynomial head coefficients. These are dense on both sides, so continuity gives \(\Psi\Phi=1\) and \(\Phi\Psi=1\text{.}\) Composing with \(Q\cong P\) proves (7.18).
🔗
For a rational refinement in the head, the two evaluation maps commute with the head restriction map. The preceding construction is therefore coefficientwise natural and gives (7.19). Presentation independence and transitivity give the same conclusion after enlarging the head.
