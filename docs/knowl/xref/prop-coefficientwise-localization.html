Proposition 7.8. Coefficientwise localisation.

Let \(\alpha\) be a rational datum whose entries lie in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\text{,}\) and put \(P=(\mathcal A^{(N)})_\alpha\text{.}\) There is a bicontinuous ring isomorphism
\begin{equation} \mathcal A_\alpha \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \left\{ \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu: p_\mu\in P,\quad \lVert p_\mu\rVert\longrightarrow0 \right\}.\tag{7.16} \end{equation}
The ring on the right has the supremum norm, and its multiplication is given by (7.14), with \(W\) replaced by its image in \(P\text{.}\) If a rational refinement is also represented by data in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) and \(P\to Q\) is the corresponding restriction map, then the induced restriction sends
\begin{equation} \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu \longmapsto \sum_\mu (p_\mu|_Q)e_\mu.\tag{7.17} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let
\begin{equation*} R=\mathcal A^{(N)}\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle, \qquad J=\overline{(gT_i-f_i)_i}\subset R, \qquad \widetilde P=R/J. \end{equation*}
Evaluation at \(T_i=f_i/g\) identifies \(\widetilde P\) with \(P\text{.}\)
🔗
The expression (7.13) continues to hold after adjoining the variables \(T_i\text{.}\) Applying the quotient map \(R\to\widetilde P\) to each coefficient gives a continuous homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \Phi:\mathcal A_\alpha\longrightarrow \left\{ \sum_\mu q_\mu e_\mu: q_\mu\in\widetilde P,\quad \lVert q_\mu\rVert\longrightarrow0 \right\}. \end{equation*}
Conversely, the canonical map from \(\widetilde P\) to \(\mathcal A_\alpha\) may be applied to every coefficient. If \(\lVert q_\mu\rVert\to0\text{,}\) the resulting series
\begin{equation*} \sum_\mu q_\mu e_\mu \end{equation*}
converges in \(\mathcal A_\alpha\text{,}\) and defines a continuous homomorphism \(\Psi\) in the other direction. The multiplication rule (7.14) shows that both maps are ring homomorphisms.
🔗
The two composites are the identity on sums with finitely many nonzero coefficients, each represented by a polynomial. Such sums are dense on both sides, so continuity gives \(\Psi\Phi=1\) and \(\Phi\Psi=1\text{.}\) Composing with \(\widetilde P\cong P\) proves (7.16).
🔗
For a rational refinement represented in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\text{,}\) the two evaluation maps commute with the restriction map \(P\to Q\text{.}\) The preceding construction therefore gives (7.17).
