Proposition 4.1.

There is a canonical isomorphism of complete topological rings
\begin{equation*} A_\alpha\cong k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X,\quad Q\longmapsto Q. \end{equation*}

Proof.
Put
\begin{equation*} T_0=k^\circ\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad T=T_0[1/\varpi]. \end{equation*}
Let \(\rho:A\to A_\alpha\) be the canonical map. We first show that
\begin{equation} Q^2C\subseteq\ker(\rho).\tag{4.1} \end{equation}
Take \(y\in Q^2C\text{,}\) and choose \(r\geq0\) such that \(\varpi^r y\in Q^2C_0\text{.}\) For every \(n\geq0\text{,}\) the coefficient description of \(A_0\) gives
\begin{equation*} W^{-n}\varpi^r y\in A_0: \end{equation*}
multiplication by \(W^{-n}\) only introduces negative powers of \(W\) in terms of \(Q\)-degree at least two. In \(A_0[X]\text{,}\) where \(W=\varpi X\text{,}\) we therefore have
\begin{equation*} \varpi^r y =W^n(W^{-n}\varpi^r y) =\varpi^nX^n(W^{-n}\varpi^r y)\in\varpi^nA_0[X] \end{equation*}
for every \(n\text{.}\) Hence \(\varpi^r y\) maps to zero in \(\widehat{A_0[X]}\text{.}\) Since \(\varpi\) is invertible in \(A_\alpha\text{,}\) this proves (4.1).
🔗
We now construct the isomorphism. Recall that the map \(\iota_B:A\to B\) from the defining pullback is given by
\begin{equation*} f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h\longmapsto f_0(W)+Qf_1(W); \end{equation*}
it simply discards the terms divisible by \(Q^2\text{.}\) The substitution \(W\mapsto\varpi X\text{,}\) \(Q\mapsto Q\) defines a continuous homomorphism \(B\to T\text{.}\) Their composite is
\begin{equation*} \theta:A\longrightarrow T,\qquad f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h\longmapsto f_0(\varpi X)+Qf_1(\varpi X). \end{equation*}
It sends \(A_0\) into \(T_0\) and sends \(W/\varpi\) to \(X\text{.}\) It therefore sends \(A_0[X]\) into \(T_0\) and extends, first to the completions and then after inverting \(\varpi\text{,}\) to a continuous homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \Phi:A_\alpha\longrightarrow T. \end{equation*}
🔗
Set
\begin{equation*} \overline X=\rho(W)/\varpi,\qquad \overline Q=\rho(Q). \end{equation*}
Both elements are power-bounded: they belong to the completed ring of definition coming from \(A_0[X]\text{.}\) Moreover, \(\overline Q^{\,2}=0\) by (4.1). Evaluation at \(\overline X\) therefore gives a continuous homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \Psi:T\longrightarrow A_\alpha,\qquad f+Qg\longmapsto f(\overline X)+g(\overline X)\overline Q. \end{equation*}
🔗
It remains to check the two composites. Write \(a=f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+y\in A\text{,}\) with \(y\in Q^2C\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} \Psi\Phi(\rho(a)) =f_0(\varpi\overline X)+\overline Qf_1(\varpi\overline X) =\rho\bigl(f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)\bigr) =\rho(a), \end{equation*}
where the last equality uses (4.1). The image of \(A\) is dense in \(A_\alpha\text{,}\) so continuity gives \(\Psi\Phi=1\text{.}\) Conversely, \(\Phi\Psi\) fixes \(k\text{,}\) \(X\) and \(Q\text{,}\) and hence is the identity on the dense subring \(k[X,Q]/(Q^2)\) of \(T\text{.}\) It follows by continuity that \(\Phi\Psi=1\text{.}\) Thus \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are inverse continuous homomorphisms.
