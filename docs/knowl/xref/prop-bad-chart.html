Proposition 4.1.

There is a canonical isomorphism of complete topological rings
\begin{equation*} A_{\mathrm{in}}\cong k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X,\quad Q\longmapsto Q. \end{equation*}

Proof.
Put
\begin{equation*} T=k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2). \end{equation*}
Take the two-jet map \(A\to B\) and then rescale its disc variable by \(W\mapsto\varpi X\text{.}\) This gives a continuous map
\begin{equation*} \theta:A\longrightarrow T,\qquad f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h\longmapsto f_0(\varpi X)+Qf_1(\varpi X). \end{equation*}
The element \(\theta(\varpi)\) is a unit and \(\theta(W)/\theta(\varpi)=X\) is power-bounded. The universal property first extends \(\theta\) to the algebraic rational localisation and then, by continuity, to a map
\begin{equation*} \Phi:A_{\mathrm{in}}\longrightarrow T. \end{equation*}
🔗
We next record why the two-jet map loses no information other than the \(Q^2\)-tail after completion. If the constant and linear \(Q\)-coefficients of \(y\in A\) vanish, then \(y_n=W^{-n}y\) still belongs to \(A\text{,}\) and the \(y_n\) have bounded norm. In \(A_{\mathrm{in}}\) one has
\begin{equation*} \overline y=\varpi^nX^n\overline{y_n}\qquad(n\geq0). \end{equation*}
The powers of \(X\) are bounded and \(\varpi^n\to0\text{,}\) so \(\overline y=0\text{.}\) In particular, the image \(\overline Q\) of \(Q\) satisfies \(\overline Q^{\,2}=0\text{.}\)
🔗
Let \(\overline X=\overline W/\varpi\text{.}\) Since \(\overline X\) is power-bounded, evaluation at \(\overline X\) gives a continuous map \(k\langle X\rangle\to A_{\mathrm{in}}\text{.}\) We may therefore define
\begin{equation*} \Psi:T\longrightarrow A_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad f+Qg\longmapsto f(\overline X)+g(\overline X)\overline Q. \end{equation*}
To check the first composite, decompose \(a\in A\) as \(a=f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+y\text{,}\) where the first two coefficients lie in \(k\langle W\rangle\) and the first two \(Q\)-coefficients of \(y\) vanish. The preceding paragraph kills \(y\text{,}\) and hence \(\Psi(\theta(a))=\overline a\text{.}\) This proves \(\Psi\Phi=1\) on the dense algebraic rational localisation, and therefore on \(A_{\mathrm{in}}\text{.}\) In the other direction, \(\Phi\Psi\) fixes constants, \(X\) and \(Q\text{.}\) It is consequently the identity first on polynomials, then on \(k\langle X\rangle\) by density, and finally on \(T\text{.}\) Thus \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are inverse continuous homomorphisms.
