Theorem 7.10.

The Huber pair \((\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\) is sheafy.
🔗
Lean
🔗

Proof.
We first prove the topological sheaf condition on the rational basis of \(X=\Spa(\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\text{.}\) Let \(U\subset X\) be rational. By Corollary 7.9, choose a head presentation
\begin{equation*} E=\mathcal O_X(U) \cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_Me_\mu, \qquad P_M=(\mathcal A_M)_\alpha, \end{equation*}
for some \(M\text{.}\) Let \(E_U^+\) be the plus ring defining the rational subspace \(U\text{;}\) it need not equal the maximal ring \(E^\circ\text{.}\) Choose the standard closed ring of definition \(P_{M,0}\subset P_M^\circ\) furnished by the head graph presentation. The coefficientwise quotient by these relations gives a closed ring of definition
\begin{equation*} E_0=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_{M,0}e_\mu\subseteq E_U^+. \end{equation*}
The inclusion holds because \(E_U^+\) is an open, hence closed, subring containing the integral graph algebra. After enlarging the head, finite tail truncation shows that the corresponding head lattices are dense in \(E_0\text{.}\)
🔗
Suppose that \(U=\bigcup_{i=1}^rU_i\) is a finite rational cover. Choose rational data for the \(U_i\) inside the Huber pair \((E,E_U^+)\text{.}\) Scale their entries into \(E_0\) and choose integral Bezout relations. After a common enlargement of the head, the density just noted and Lemma 7.7 replace all these data by data lying in one head lattice \(P_{M,0}\text{,}\) without changing any \(U_i\) or its completed section ring. Applying the coefficient projection \(E\to P_M\) to the integral Bezout relation supplied by Lemma 7.7 shows that each new datum is rational already in \(P_M\text{.}\) Let
\begin{equation*} V_i\subset\Spa(P_M,P_M^\circ) \end{equation*}
be the corresponding rational domains.
🔗
We verify that the \(V_i\) cover. The inclusion \(P_M\to E\) and coefficient projection \(E\to P_M\) are norm-nonincreasing and split each other. They therefore define morphisms of the maximal Huber pairs and a split surjection
\begin{equation} \Spa(E,E^\circ)\longrightarrow\Spa(P_M,P_M^\circ).\tag{7.29} \end{equation}
The maximal-pair spectrum \(\Spa(E,E^\circ)\) is a subspace of \(\Spa(E,E_U^+)=U\text{.}\) Hence the given cover restricts to a cover of \(\Spa(E,E^\circ)\text{.}\) On this subspace the restricted \(U_i\) are exactly the inverse images of the \(V_i\text{.}\) The surjectivity in (7.29) therefore implies that the \(V_i\) cover \(\Spa(P_M,P_M^\circ)\text{.}\) This avoids identifying \(U\) with the maximal-pair spectrum, which need not be correct at higher-rank points.
🔗
Write
\begin{equation*} P_{M,i}=\mathcal O(V_i), \qquad P_{M,ij}=\mathcal O(V_i\cap V_j). \end{equation*}
Naturality in Proposition 7.8 gives canonical identifications
\begin{equation} \mathcal O_X(U_i) \cong\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_{M,i}e_\mu, \qquad \mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j) \cong\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_{M,ij}e_\mu,\tag{7.30} \end{equation}
under which every restriction map is coefficientwise.
🔗
The affinoid algebra \(P_M\) is sheafy, so its rational-cover equalizer is an isomorphism of Banach spaces
\begin{equation} P_M\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \operatorname{Eq}\left( \prod_iP_{M,i}\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}P_{M,ij} \right).\tag{7.31} \end{equation}
Its inverse is bounded; fix a bound \(C\text{.}\) Let \((x_i)_i\) be a matching family in the section rings in (7.30), and write \(x_i=\sum_\mu x_{i,\mu}e_\mu\text{.}\) For each \(\mu\text{,}\) equation (7.31) glues \((x_{i,\mu})_i\) to a unique \(x_\mu\in P_M\text{,}\) with
\begin{equation} \lVert x_\mu\rVert \leq C\max_i\lVert x_{i,\mu}\rVert.\tag{7.32} \end{equation}
Because the cover is finite and every coefficient family is null, the right side tends to zero. Thus \(\sum_\mu x_\mu e_\mu\) is an actual element of the \(c_0\)-sum and gives the unique global section. The same bound proves that the restriction map is a topological embedding. Hence the structure presheaf satisfies the sheaf condition as complete topological rings on the rational basis.
🔗
For an arbitrary open cover, apply the projective-limit argument used in the last paragraph of the proof of Lemma 6.1. Namely, every rational subdomain of the open set is quasi-compact and admits a finite rational cover subordinate to the given cover; the rational-basis result glues continuously on that subdomain, and uniqueness makes these sections compatible under rational restriction. The defining projective-limit topology on arbitrary-open sections then makes the inverse gluing map continuous. Thus the public all-open structure presheaf is a sheaf of complete topological rings.
