Proposition 8.4.

If \(w(n)\geq1\) for every \(n\geq1\text{,}\) then \(\mathcal A_w\) is not noetherian.

🔗

Proof.
Assume that \(w(n)\geq1\) for every \(n\geq1\text{.}\) For \(m\geq1\text{,}\) let
\begin{equation*} I_m=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m)\subset\mathcal A. \end{equation*}
We show that \(Z_{m+1}\notin I_m\text{.}\)
🔗
Let \(e_{m+1}\) denote the multi-index supported at \(m+1\text{,}\) with value \(1\) there. Define
\begin{equation*} \psi_m:\mathcal A\longrightarrow k\langle T\rangle \end{equation*}
by the formula
\begin{equation*} \psi_m\left(\sum_{a,\nu}c_{a,\nu}W^aU^\nu\right) =\sum_{q\geq0}c_{0,2q e_{m+1}}T^q. \end{equation*}
This is well defined because the retained coefficients still tend to zero.
🔗
We claim that \(\psi_m\) is multiplicative. A monomial in \(\mathcal A\) which involves no \(W\) and no \(U_j\) for \(j\neq m+1\) must be of the form \(U_{m+1}^{2q}\text{.}\) Indeed, an odd exponent of \(U_{m+1}\) would contribute \(w(m+1)\geq1\) to its weight and would therefore require a positive power of \(W\text{.}\) Since all exponents are nonnegative, a product can contribute \(U_{m+1}^{2q}\) only when the two monomials are \(U_{m+1}^{2q_1}\) and \(U_{m+1}^{2q_2}\text{,}\) with \(q_1+q_2=q\text{.}\) It follows that
\begin{equation*} \psi_m(fg)=\psi_m(f)\psi_m(g). \end{equation*}
🔗
For \(1\leq j\leq m\text{,}\) we have \(\psi_m(Z_j)=0\text{,}\) whereas \(\psi_m(Z_{m+1})=T\neq0\text{.}\) Thus \(\psi_m\) vanishes on \(I_m\) but not on \(Z_{m+1}\text{,}\) so \(Z_{m+1}\notin I_m\text{.}\) Since \(Z_{m+1}\in I_{m+1}\text{,}\) we obtain a strictly increasing chain
\begin{equation*} I_1\subsetneq I_2\subsetneq I_3\subsetneq\cdots, \end{equation*}
and therefore \(\mathcal A\) is not noetherian.
