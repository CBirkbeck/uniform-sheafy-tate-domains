Proposition 7.4.

The ring \(\mathcal A\) is not noetherian.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
For \(m\geq1\text{,}\) let
\begin{equation*} I_m=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m)\subset\mathcal A. \end{equation*}
There is a compatible family of norm-nonincreasing maps on the finite stages, and hence a bounded homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \psi_m:\mathcal A\longrightarrow k\langle T\rangle, \end{equation*}
which sends \(W\) and every \(Y_j\) to zero, sends \(Z_{m+1}\) to \(T\text{,}\) and sends every other \(Z_j\) to zero. The relations in (7.7) are preserved. The map \(\psi_m\) kills \(I_m\) but not \(Z_{m+1}\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{equation*} I_1\subsetneq I_2\subsetneq I_3\subsetneq\cdots, \end{equation*}
so \(\mathcal A\) is not noetherian.
