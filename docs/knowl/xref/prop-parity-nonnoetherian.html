Proposition 7.4.

The ring \(\mathcal A\) is not noetherian.
🔗
Lean
🔗

Proof.
For \(m\geq1\text{,}\) let
\begin{equation*} I_m=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m)\subset\mathcal A. \end{equation*}
Define a norm-nonincreasing homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \psi_m:\mathcal A\longrightarrow k\langle T\rangle, \end{equation*}
by retaining only the coefficients of the monomials \(U_{m+1}^{2q}\) and sending that monomial to \(T^q\text{.}\) The support condition shows directly that this is multiplicative: a product can contribute such a monomial only when both factors contribute monomials of the same form. Thus \(\psi_m\) sends \(W\) and every \(Y_j\) to zero, sends \(Z_{m+1}\) to \(T\text{,}\) and sends every other \(Z_j\) to zero. It kills \(I_m\) but not \(Z_{m+1}\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{equation*} I_1\subsetneq I_2\subsetneq I_3\subsetneq\cdots, \end{equation*}
so \(\mathcal A\) is not noetherian.
