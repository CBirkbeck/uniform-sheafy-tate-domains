Proposition 7.4.

The ring \(\mathcal A\) is a complete uniform Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain, with
\begin{equation*} \mathcal A^\circ=\mathcal A_0. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The Gauss norm on the countable restricted Tate algebra \(k\langle W,U_1,U_2,\ldots\rangle\) is multiplicative. Indeed, every nonzero restricted series attains its norm. After scaling two nonzero series to norm one, reduction modulo \(\varpi\) leaves two nonzero polynomials in \(\widetilde k[W,U_1,U_2,\ldots]\text{,}\) and their product is nonzero. Scaling back proves multiplicativity. The support algebra \(\mathcal A\) embeds isometrically into this Tate algebra by (7.5), so its Gauss norm is also multiplicative and it is a domain.
🔗
If \(x\in\mathcal A_0\text{,}\) all powers of \(x\) remain in \(\mathcal A_0\text{.}\) If \(x\notin\mathcal A_0\text{,}\) multiplicativity gives \(\lVert x^m\rVert_G=\lVert x\rVert_G^m\text{,}\) which is unbounded. Hence \(\mathcal A^\circ=\mathcal A_0\text{.}\) Completeness and the Tate property follow from (7.5) and the topologically nilpotent unit \(\varpi\text{.}\)
