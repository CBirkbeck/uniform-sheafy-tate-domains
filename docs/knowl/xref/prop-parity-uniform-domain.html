Proposition 8.3.

For every weight \(w\text{,}\) the ring \(\mathcal A_w\) is a complete uniform Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain, with
\begin{equation*} \mathcal A_w^\circ=\mathcal A_{w,0}. \end{equation*}

Proof.
Let \(f\) and \(g\) be nonzero elements of \(k\langle W,U_1,U_2,\ldots\rangle\text{.}\) Since their coefficients tend to zero, each Gauss norm is attained by a coefficient. After dividing \(f\) and \(g\) by coefficients of maximal norm, we may assume that
\begin{equation*} \lVert f\rVert_G=\lVert g\rVert_G=1. \end{equation*}
Only finitely many coefficients of either series then have norm \(1\text{.}\) Consequently, reduction modulo the maximal ideal of \(k^\circ\) gives nonzero polynomials
\begin{equation*} \overline f,\overline g \in \widetilde{k}[W,U_1,U_2,\ldots]. \end{equation*}
This polynomial ring is an integral domain, so \(\overline f\,\overline g\neq0\text{.}\) Reduction commutes with multiplication: for each monomial, its coefficient in a product is a finite sum. Hence some coefficient of \(fg\) has norm \(1\text{,}\) and therefore
\begin{equation*} \lVert fg\rVert_G\geq1. \end{equation*}
The reverse inequality follows from submultiplicativity of the Gauss norm. Thus \(\lVert fg\rVert_G=1\text{,}\) and rescaling gives
\begin{equation*} \lVert fg\rVert_G=\lVert f\rVert_G\lVert g\rVert_G \end{equation*}
in general.
🔗
The algebra \(\mathcal A\) is a closed subring of this restricted Tate algebra, and its norm is the restriction of the Gauss norm. Its norm is therefore multiplicative, and \(\mathcal A\) is an integral domain.
🔗
If \(x\in\mathcal A_0\text{,}\) then \(\lVert x\rVert_G\leq1\text{,}\) so every power of \(x\) also lies in \(\mathcal A_0\text{.}\) If \(x\notin\mathcal A_0\text{,}\) then \(\lVert x\rVert_G>1\text{,}\) and multiplicativity gives
\begin{equation*} \lVert x^m\rVert_G=\lVert x\rVert_G^m, \end{equation*}
which is unbounded. Hence \(\mathcal A^\circ=\mathcal A_0\text{.}\) The subring \(\mathcal A_0\) is the norm unit ball and hence bounded, so \(\mathcal A\) is uniform. Completeness and the Tate property follow from (8.5) and the topologically nilpotent unit \(\varpi\text{.}\)
