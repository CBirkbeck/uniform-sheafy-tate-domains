Lemma 5.1.

The Koszul complex \(K_{P_E}(r_1,\dots,r_m)\) is exact in positive degrees. Every differential is continuous and strict onto its image, and every image is closed. In particular, if
\begin{equation*} d_{1,E}:P_E^m\longrightarrow P_E, \qquad J_E=\im(d_{1,E}), \end{equation*}
then \(J_E\) is closed. Moreover, there exists \(h_E\geq 0\) such that
\begin{equation} \varpi^{h_E}(J_E\cap P_{E,0}) \subset d_{1,E}(P_{E,0}^m).\tag{5.1} \end{equation}
There also exists \(z_E\geq 0\) such that
\begin{equation} \varpi^{z_E}\bigl(\ker(d_{1,E})\cap P_{E,0}^m\bigr) \subset d_{2,E}\bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge^2 P_{E,0}^m\bigr).\tag{5.2} \end{equation}
Here positive-degree exactness means \(\operatorname{Exact}(d_{q+1,E},d_{q,E})\) for every \(q\geq0\text{,}\) equivalently exactness at the term of exterior degree \(q+1\text{.}\) It does not assert exactness at exterior degree zero and does not assert that \(d_{1,E}\) is surjective onto \(P_E\text{.}\) For \(m=0\text{,}\) the positive-degree assertion is vacuous.

Proof.
First work over \(E[T_1,\dots,T_m]\text{.}\) Let \(\mathfrak p\) be a prime which does not contain all the \(r_i\text{,}\) and choose \(j\) with \(r_j\notin\mathfrak p\text{.}\) Then \(r_j\) is a unit after localization at \(\mathfrak p\text{.}\) If \(e_1,\dots,e_m\) is the standard basis used to form the Koszul complex and \(d\) is the Koszul differential, define a degree-\(1\) homotopy by
\begin{equation*} h(\omega)=r_j^{-1}e_j\wedge\omega. \end{equation*}
The standard Koszul identity
\begin{equation*} d(e_j\wedge\omega)=r_j\omega-e_j\wedge d(\omega) \end{equation*}
gives
\begin{equation*} dh+hd=\operatorname{id}. \end{equation*}
Thus the identity chain map is chain-homotopic to the zero map; this is what it means here for the localized complex to be contractible. In particular it is exact: if \(d\omega=0\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} \omega=(dh+hd)(\omega)=d(h\omega), \end{equation*}
so every cycle is a boundary. Now let \(\mathfrak p\) contain every \(r_i\text{,}\) and choose \(1=a_0g+\sum_i a_if_i\text{.}\) In the local ring,
\begin{equation*} g\left(a_0+\sum_i a_iT_i\right)=1+\sum_i a_ir_i. \end{equation*}
The right-hand side is a unit, so \(g\) is a unit. After translating \(T_i\) by \(f_i/g\text{,}\) the sequence \((r_i)\) differs by units from the coordinate sequence \((T_i)\text{,}\) which is regular over an arbitrary coefficient ring. Hence the polynomial Koszul complex is exact in positive degrees.
🔗
The ring \(E_0\) is noetherian and \(\varpi\)-adically complete. More precisely, the canonical map
\begin{equation*} E[T_1,\ldots,T_m]=E_0[T_1,\ldots,T_m][1/\varpi] \longrightarrow E_0\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle[1/\varpi]=P_E \end{equation*}
is flat: \(E_0[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\) is noetherian, its \(\varpi\)-adic completion \(E_0\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle\) is flat over it by noetherian adic-completion flatness [bib-Stacks, Tag 00MB], and localization at \(\varpi\) preserves flatness. Flat base change therefore carries the positive-degree polynomial Koszul exactness to \(P_E\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally, \(P_E\) is a complete noetherian Tate ring. Huber’s finite-module results imply that homomorphisms between finite \(P_E\)-modules are strict and that their images are closed [bib-Huber94, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4]. Applying the bounded-denominator criterion (2.1) to \(d_{1,E}\) and to \(d_{2,E}\) onto \(\ker(d_{1,E})\) gives (5.1) and (5.2).
