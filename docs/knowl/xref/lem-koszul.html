Lemma 5.1.

The Koszul complex
\begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow \bigwedge^mT_E^m\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow\bigwedge^2T_E^m\xrightarrow{d_{2,E}} T_E^m\xrightarrow{d_{1,E}}T_E \end{equation*}
is exact in positive degrees. Every differential is continuous and strict onto its image, and every image is closed. In particular, if
\begin{equation*} I_E=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)=\im(d_{1,E})\subset T_E, \qquad d_{1,E}(u_1,\ldots,u_m)=\sum_i u_ir_i, \end{equation*}
then \(I_E\) is closed. Moreover, there exists \(h_E\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) such that
\begin{equation} \varpi^{h_E}(I_E\cap T_{E,0}) \subset d_{1,E}(T_{E,0}^m).\tag{5.1} \end{equation}
There also exists \(z_E\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) such that
\begin{equation} \varpi^{z_E}\bigl(\ker(d_{1,E})\cap T_{E,0}^m\bigr) \subset d_{2,E}\bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge^2 T_{E,0}^m\bigr).\tag{5.2} \end{equation}

Proof.
Over the polynomial ring \(E[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\text{,}\) the sequence \(gT_i-f_i\) has Koszul complex exact in positive degrees. Indeed, this may be checked after localising at a prime. If one of the \(gT_i-f_i\) is not in the prime, it is a unit after localisation and the Koszul complex is contractible. If all of them lie in the prime, then \(g\) does not: otherwise all the \(f_i\) would also lie in the prime, contrary to \((g,f_1,\ldots,f_m)=E\text{.}\) Thus \(g\) is invertible there and
\begin{equation*} gT_i-f_i=g(T_i-f_i/g). \end{equation*}
The elements \(T_i-f_i/g\) form a regular sequence, since they are monic linear polynomials in distinct variables. This proves the assertion over the polynomial ring.
🔗
The map
\begin{equation*} E[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\longrightarrow T_E \end{equation*}
is flat. In fact, \(E_0\) is noetherian for \(E=B,C,D\text{,}\) so \(E_0[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\) is noetherian and its \(\varpi\)-adic completion is flat over it [bib-Stacks, Tag 00MB]. Inverting \(\varpi\) gives the displayed flat map. Tensoring the polynomial Koszul complex with \(T_E\text{,}\) and using the standard base-change identification of its finite free terms, proves positive-degree exactness. Notice that this does not assert exactness in degree zero.
🔗
Each differential is continuous because its coordinates are finite sums of multiplication maps. The image of \(d_{1,E}\) is closed by the noetherian closed-ideal theorem. In higher degree, exactness identifies an image with the kernel of the next continuous differential, and hence that image is closed. The nonarchimedean open mapping theorem therefore shows that, for each differential \(d_{q,E}\text{,}\) there is a constant \(C_{q,E}\geq1\) such that every \(y\in\im(d_{q,E})\) has a preimage \(x\) satisfying
\begin{equation*} d_{q,E}(x)=y, \qquad \lVert x\rVert\leq C_{q,E}\lVert y\rVert. \end{equation*}
This also proves that each differential is strict onto its image.
🔗
Choose \(h_E\geq0\) such that \(|\varpi|^{h_E}C_{1,E}\leq1\text{.}\) If \(x\in I_E\cap T_{E,0}\text{,}\) choose \(u\in T_E^m\) with
\begin{equation*} d_{1,E}(u)=x, \qquad \lVert u\rVert\leq C_{1,E}\lVert x\rVert\leq C_{1,E}. \end{equation*}
Then \(\varpi^{h_E}u\in T_{E,0}^m\) and \(d_{1,E}(\varpi^{h_E}u)=\varpi^{h_E}x\text{,}\) which proves (5.1). Similarly, choose \(z_E\geq0\) such that \(|\varpi|^{z_E}C_{2,E}\leq1\text{.}\) If \(u\in\ker(d_{1,E})\cap T_{E,0}^m\text{,}\) choose \(v\) with \(d_{2,E}(v)=u\) and \(\lVert v\rVert\leq C_{2,E}\lVert u\rVert\text{.}\) Then \(\varpi^{z_E}v\in\bigwedge^2T_{E,0}^m\text{,}\) proving (5.2).
