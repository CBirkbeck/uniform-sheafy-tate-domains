Lemma 5.1.

The Koszul complex
\begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow \bigwedge^mT_E^m\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow\bigwedge^2T_E^m\xrightarrow{d_{2,E}} T_E^m\xrightarrow{d_{1,E}}T_E \end{equation*}
is exact in positive degrees. Every differential is continuous and strict onto its image, and every image is closed. In particular, if
\begin{equation*} I_E=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)=\im(d_{1,E})\subset T_E, \qquad d_{1,E}(u_1,\ldots,u_m)=\sum_i u_ir_i, \end{equation*}
then \(I_E\) is closed. Moreover, there exists \(h_E\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}\) such that
\begin{equation} \varpi^{h_E}(I_E\cap T_{E,0}) \subset d_{1,E}(T_{E,0}^m).\tag{5.1} \end{equation}
There also exists \(z_E\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}\) such that
\begin{equation} \varpi^{z_E}\bigl(\ker(d_{1,E})\cap T_{E,0}^m\bigr) \subset d_{2,E}\bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge^2 T_{E,0}^m\bigr).\tag{5.2} \end{equation}

Proof.
Positive-degree exactness follows from the strict Koszul regularity result cited above. Since \(T_E\) is a complete Tate ring with noetherian ring of definition \(T_{E,0}\text{,}\) Huber’s finite-module lemmas show that the differentials are continuous and strict and that their images are closed [bib-Huber94, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4]. Apply (2.1) to \(d_{1,E}:T_E^m\to I_E\text{,}\) using the target lattice \(I_E\cap T_{E,0}\text{.}\) Exactness identifies \(\im(d_{2,E})=\ker(d_{1,E})\text{;}\) applying the same criterion to \(d_{2,E}\) with target lattice \(\ker(d_{1,E})\cap T_{E,0}^m\) gives (5.1) and (5.2).
