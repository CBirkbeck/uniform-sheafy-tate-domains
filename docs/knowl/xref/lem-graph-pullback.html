Lemma 5.2.

The ideal \(I_A\) is closed in \(T_A\text{,}\) and the canonical map
\begin{equation*} I_A\longrightarrow I_B\times_{I_D}I_C \end{equation*}
is an isomorphism. Give each \(I_E\) the subspace topology inherited from \(T_E\text{,}\) and give \(I_B\oplus I_C\) the max norm. Then the sequence of topological \(k\)-vector spaces
\begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow I_A\longrightarrow I_B\oplus I_C \longrightarrow I_D\longrightarrow0\tag{5.4} \end{equation}
is strict exact. Here the maps are
\begin{equation*} x\longmapsto(x_B,x_C), \qquad (x_B,x_C)\longmapsto (x_B)_D-(x_C)_D. \end{equation*}

Proof.
For \(E=B,C,D\text{,}\) let \(C_{1,E}\) be as in the proof of Lemma 5.1, and let \(C_{2,D}\) be the corresponding constant for \(d_{2,D}\text{.}\) Take \((x_B,x_C)\in I_B\times_{I_D}I_C\text{,}\) and choose \(u_B\in T_B^m\) and \(u_C\in T_C^m\) such that
\begin{equation*} d_{1,B}(u_B)=x_B,\qquad \lVert u_B\rVert\leq C_{1,B}\lVert x_B\rVert, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} d_{1,C}(u_C)=x_C,\qquad \lVert u_C\rVert\leq C_{1,C}\lVert x_C\rVert. \end{equation*}
In \(T_D^m\) the difference
\begin{equation*} w=(u_B)_D-(u_C)_D \end{equation*}
belongs to \(\ker(d_{1,D})\text{.}\) Choose \(v_D\in\bigwedge^2T_D^m\) such that
\begin{equation*} d_{2,D}(v_D)=w,\qquad \lVert v_D\rVert\leq C_{2,D}\lVert w\rVert. \end{equation*}
The norm-preserving coefficientwise section \(T_D\to T_C\) induces a norm-preserving section on second exterior powers; use it to obtain \(v_C\in\bigwedge^2T_C^m\) mapping to \(v_D\text{.}\) Replacing \(u_C\) by
\begin{equation*} u_C'=u_C+d_{2,C}(v_C) \end{equation*}
does not change \(d_{1,C}(u_C)\text{,}\) and the images of \(u_B\) and \(u_C'\) in \(T_D^m\) now agree. Exactness of (5.3), applied coefficientwise, gives \(u_A\in T_A^m\) mapping to this pair. Then \(d_{1,A}(u_A)\) maps to \((x_B,x_C)\text{.}\)
🔗
These bounds, continuity of \(d_{2,C}\text{,}\) and the norm-preserving coefficientwise section give a constant \(C\text{,}\) independent of \((x_B,x_C)\text{,}\) for which \(x_A=d_{1,A}(u_A)\) satisfies
\begin{equation*} \lVert x_A\rVert\leq C\max\{\lVert x_B\rVert,\lVert x_C\rVert\}. \end{equation*}
The map \(T_A\to T_B\oplus T_C\) is injective, so this proves both the algebraic pullback assertion and the boundedness of its inverse.
🔗
It remains to see that \(I_A\) is closed. The ideals \(I_B\) and \(I_C\) are closed by Lemma 5.1. If \(x\) lies in the closure of \(I_A\text{,}\) its images lie in \(I_B\) and \(I_C\text{.}\) The pullback isomorphism just proved gives \(x_A\in I_A\) with the same two images as \(x\text{.}\) Injectivity of \(T_A\to T_B\oplus T_C\) gives \(x=x_A\text{,}\) as required.
🔗
Finally, for \(y\in I_D\) choose \(u_D\in T_D^m\) such that
\begin{equation*} d_{1,D}(u_D)=y,\qquad \lVert u_D\rVert\leq C_{1,D}\lVert y\rVert. \end{equation*}
Apply the norm-preserving section \(T_D^m\to T_C^m\) and then \(d_{1,C}\text{.}\) This gives \(x_C\in I_C\) mapping to \(y\text{,}\) with \(\lVert x_C\rVert\) bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\lVert y\rVert\text{.}\) Thus \(I_B\oplus I_C\to I_D\) is a strict surjection. Its kernel is \(I_A\) by the pullback assertion, and the preceding bound for the inverse \(I_B\times_{I_D}I_C\to I_A\) makes the inclusion on the left strict. This proves (5.4).
