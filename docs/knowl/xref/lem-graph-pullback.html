Lemma 5.2.

The ideal \(I_A\) is closed in \(T_A\text{,}\) and the canonical map
\begin{equation*} I_A\longrightarrow I_B\times_{I_D}I_C \end{equation*}
is an isomorphism. Moreover,
\begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow I_A\longrightarrow I_B\oplus I_C \longrightarrow I_D\longrightarrow 0\tag{5.4} \end{equation}
is strict exact.

Proof.
We first prove the algebraic statement. Let \((x_B,x_C)\in I_B\times_{I_D}I_C\text{.}\) Choose \(u_B\in T_B^m\) and \(u_C\in T_C^m\) with \(d_{1,B}(u_B)=x_B\) and \(d_{1,C}(u_C)=x_C\text{.}\) Their difference in \(T_D^m\) lies in \(\ker(d_{1,D})\text{.}\) By Lemma 5.1, it is \(d_{2,D}(v_D)\) for some \(v_D\in\bigwedge^2T_D^m\text{.}\) Lift \(v_D\) to \(v_C\in\bigwedge^2T_C^m\) and replace \(u_C\) by \(u_C+d_{2,C}(v_C)\text{.}\) The new pair of coefficient vectors has the same image in \(T_D^m\text{,}\) hence comes from \(u_A\in T_A^m\) by (5.3). Then \(d_{1,A}(u_A)\) maps to \((x_B,x_C)\text{.}\) This proves the asserted algebraic isomorphism.
🔗
We now make this correction with bounded denominators. The exponents \(h_B\) and \(h_C\) make the coefficient lifts integral, whilst \(z_D\) makes the syzygy correction integral. Put \(h=\max\{h_B,h_C\}\text{,}\) and suppose that \((x_B,x_C)\) belongs to the pullback and lies in \(T_{B,0}\oplus T_{C,0}\text{.}\) Choose \(u_B\in T_{B,0}^m\) and \(u_C\in T_{C,0}^m\) such that
\begin{equation*} d_{1,B}(u_B)=\varpi^h x_B, \qquad d_{1,C}(u_C)=\varpi^h x_C. \end{equation*}
The difference \(s=(u_B)_D-(u_C)_D\) belongs to \(\ker(d_{1,D})\cap T_{D,0}^m\text{.}\) By (5.2), choose \(v_D\in\bigwedge^2T_{D,0}^m\) with \(d_{2,D}(v_D)=\varpi^{z_D}s\text{.}\) Lift \(v_D\) to \(v_C\in\bigwedge^2T_{C,0}^m\) and set
\begin{equation*} u_C'=\varpi^{z_D}u_C+d_{2,C}(v_C). \end{equation*}
Then \((u_C')_D=\varpi^{z_D}(u_B)_D\) and \(d_{1,C}(u_C')=\varpi^{h+z_D}x_C\text{.}\) By (5.3), the compatible integral pair \((\varpi^{z_D}u_B,u_C')\) comes from \(u_A\in T_{A,0}^m\text{.}\) Thus
\begin{equation} \varpi^{h+z_D}\bigl((I_B\times_{I_D}I_C) \cap(T_{B,0}\oplus T_{C,0})\bigr) \subset d_{1,A}(T_{A,0}^m).\tag{5.5} \end{equation}
This proves strictness of the pullback identification. The fibre product \(I_B\times_{I_D}I_C\) is closed in \(T_B\oplus T_C\text{,}\) and (5.3) identifies \(T_A\) strictly with the kernel of \(T_B\oplus T_C\to T_D\text{.}\) It follows from (5.5) that \(I_A\) is closed in \(T_A\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally, the map \(I_B\oplus I_C\to I_D\) is surjective: lift a coefficient vector in \(T_D^m\) to \(T_C^m\) and apply \(d_1\text{.}\) If the element of \(I_D\) is integral, then (5.1) for \(E=D\) shows that a fixed power of \(\varpi\) has an integral coefficient vector, which can be lifted to \(T_{C,0}^m\text{.}\) Thus this surjection is strict. Together with (5.5), this proves strict exactness of (5.4).
