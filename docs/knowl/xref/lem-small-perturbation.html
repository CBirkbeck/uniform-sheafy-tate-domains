Lemma 7.7. Small perturbation lemma.

Let \(E\) be a complete nonarchimedean normed commutative ring, let \(E^+\subset E\) be a plus ring containing the closed unit ball \(E_0=\{x:\lVert x\rVert\leq1\}\text{,}\) and let \(t\in E^\times\) satisfy
\begin{equation*} 0<\lVert t\rVert<1,\qquad \lVert tx\rVert=\lVert t\rVert\lVert x\rVert\quad(x\in E). \end{equation*}
Let \(\alpha=(f_1,\ldots,f_m;g)\) be a rational datum with entries in \(E_0\text{.}\) Write \(d_0=g\) and \(d_i=f_i\) for \(i\geq1\text{.}\) Suppose that for some \(\ell\geq0\) there are \(a_0,\ldots,a_m\in E_0\) with
\begin{equation} t^\ell=\sum_{j=0}^m a_jd_j.\tag{7.12} \end{equation}
Let \(d_j'\in E_0\) satisfy
\begin{equation} \lVert d_j'-d_j\rVert\leq\lVert t\rVert^{\ell+1} \qquad(0\leq j\leq m),\tag{7.13} \end{equation}
and write \(\alpha'=(f_1',\ldots,f_m';g')\text{.}\) Then \(\alpha'\) is a rational datum, \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha'\) define the same rational subset of \(\Spa(E,E^+)\text{,}\) and their completed rational localisations are bicontinuously isomorphic by an isomorphism commuting with the canonical maps from \(E\text{.}\)

Proof.
The scaling hypothesis and the fact that \(t\) is a unit give \(d_j'-d_j=t^{\ell+1}h_j\) with \(\lVert h_j\rVert\leq1\text{.}\) Hence
\begin{equation*} \sum_j a_jd_j' =t^\ell(1+tH), \qquad H=\sum_j a_jh_j,\quad \lVert H\rVert\leq1. \end{equation*}
Since \(\lVert tH\rVert<1\text{,}\) the element \(1+tH\) is a unit with inverse in \(E_0\text{.}\) Multiplying the \(a_j\) by this inverse gives a unit-ball Bezout relation for the primed datum; in particular, \(\alpha'\) is rational.
🔗
Let \(x\) lie in the rational subset defined by \(\alpha\text{.}\) Since \(a_j\in E_0\subseteq E^+\text{,}\) relation (7.12) gives
\begin{equation*} |t(x)|^\ell\leq |g(x)|. \end{equation*}
The perturbations have value at most \(|t(x)|^{\ell+1}<|g(x)|\text{,}\) so \(|g'(x)|=|g(x)|\) and \(|f_i'(x)|\leq|g'(x)|\text{.}\) This proves one inclusion of rational subsets. Applying the same argument to the primed Bezout relation proves the reverse inclusion.
🔗
Rational localisation is independent of the datum used to present a rational subset. The equality of the two subsets therefore gives restriction maps in both directions between \(E_\alpha\) and \(E_{\alpha'}\text{.}\) Transitivity of rational restriction shows that their composites are the identity. The two restriction maps are continuous and commute with the canonical maps from \(E\text{,}\) as required.
