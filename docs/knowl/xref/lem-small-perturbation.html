Lemma 7.8. Small perturbation lemma.

Let \((E,E^+)\) be a complete Tate Huber pair and choose a closed ring of definition \(E_0\subseteq E^+\text{.}\) Let \(\alpha=(f_1,\ldots,f_m;g)\) be a rational datum whose entries lie in \(E_0\text{.}\) Write \(d_0=g\) and \(d_i=f_i\) for \(i\geq1\text{.}\) Suppose that for some \(\ell\geq0\) there are \(a_0,\ldots,a_m\in E_0\) with
\begin{equation} \varpi^\ell=\sum_{j=0}^m a_jd_j.\tag{7.19} \end{equation}
Let \(d_j'\in E_0\) satisfy
\begin{equation} d_j'-d_j\in\varpi^{\ell+1}E_0 \qquad(0\leq j\leq m),\tag{7.20} \end{equation}
and write \(\alpha'=(f_1',\ldots,f_m';g')\text{.}\) Then \(\alpha'\) is a rational datum, \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha'\) define the same rational subset of \(\Spa(E,E^+)\text{,}\) and their completed rational localizations are canonically isomorphic.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Write \(d_j'-d_j=\varpi^{\ell+1}h_j\) with \(h_j\in E_0\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} \sum_j a_jd_j' =\varpi^\ell(1+\varpi H), \qquad H=\sum_j a_jh_j\in E_0. \end{equation*}
The element \(1+\varpi H\) is a unit of \(E_0\text{,}\) with inverse given by its convergent geometric series. Multiplying the \(a_j\) by this inverse gives an integral Bezout relation for the primed datum; in particular, \(\alpha'\) is open.
🔗
Let \(x\) lie in the rational subset defined by \(\alpha\text{.}\) Since \(a_j\in E_0\subseteq E^+\text{,}\) relation (7.19) gives
\begin{equation*} |\varpi|^\ell\leq |g(x)|. \end{equation*}
The perturbations have value at most \(|\varpi|^{\ell+1}<|g(x)|\text{,}\) so \(|g'(x)|=|g(x)|\) and \(|f_i'(x)|\leq|g'(x)|\text{.}\) This proves one inclusion of rational subsets. Applying the same argument to the primed Bezout relation proves the reverse inclusion.
🔗
Let \(E_\alpha\) be the first rational localization and put
\begin{equation*} q=\frac{\varpi^\ell}{g} =a_0+\sum_i a_i\frac{f_i}{g}\in E_\alpha^\circ. \end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*} \frac{g'}g=1+\varpi h_0q. \end{equation*}
The second summand is topologically nilpotent. After adjoining the finitely many power-bounded elements involved to a ring of definition, the geometric series for its inverse and all powers of that inverse lie in the same bounded subring. Thus \(g'/g\) is a \(1\)-unit with power-bounded inverse, and
\begin{equation*} \frac{f_i'}{g'} =\frac{f_i/g+\varpi h_iq}{1+\varpi h_0q} \end{equation*}
is power-bounded. The universal property gives a homomorphism \(E_{\alpha'}\to E_\alpha\text{.}\) Repeating the argument with the primed Bezout relation gives a homomorphism in the opposite direction. Both composites restrict to the identity on \(E\text{;}\) universal-property uniqueness makes them inverse. The isomorphism is therefore canonical.
