Lemma 6.1. Sheaf transfer.

Let
\begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} R&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
be a commutative square of complete Tate Huber pairs. For \(E\in\{B,C,D\}\text{,}\) write
\begin{equation*} p_E:X_E=\Spa(E,E^+)\longrightarrow X_R=\Spa(R,R^+) \end{equation*}
for the induced map. For a rational domain \(U\subset X_R\text{,}\) put \(U_E=p_E^{-1}(U)\text{,}\) and set \(U_R=U\text{.}\) Suppose that for every rational domain \(U\subset X_R\) the section rings satisfy a natural strict exact sequence
\begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow \cO_R(U)\longrightarrow \cO_B(U_B)\oplus\cO_C(U_C) \longrightarrow\cO_D(U_D)\longrightarrow 0.\tag{6.1} \end{equation}
If the Huber pairs associated with \(B,C,D\) are sheafy as complete topological rings, then the Huber pair associated with \(R\) is sheafy as a complete topological ring.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
We first work on the rational basis. Let \(U=\bigcup_{i=1}^nU_i\) be a finite rational covering. For \(E\in\{R,B,C,D\}\text{,}\) let
\begin{equation*} r_E:\cO_E(U_E)\longrightarrow\prod_i\cO_E((U_i)_E) \end{equation*}
denote product restriction, using \(U_R=U\) and \((U_i)_R=U_i\) as specified in the statement. The first map in (6.1) is a topological embedding, and the same is true after replacing \(U\) by any \(U_i\text{.}\) Since \(B\) and \(C\) are sheafy, \(r_B\) and \(r_C\) are topological embeddings. Naturality gives a commutative square
\begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} \cO_R(U)&\longrightarrow&\cO_B(U_B)\oplus\cO_C(U_C)\\ \downarrow r_R&&\downarrow r_B\oplus r_C\\ \prod_i\cO_R(U_i)&\longrightarrow& \prod_i\bigl(\cO_B((U_i)_B)\oplus\cO_C((U_i)_C)\bigr). \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
The top and right arrows are topological embeddings, as is the bottom arrow. It follows that \(r_R\) is a topological embedding.
🔗
Now let \(x_i\in\cO_R(U_i)\) be a matching family. Its images in the \(B\)- and \(C\)-section rings glue to elements
\begin{equation*} b\in\cO_B(U_B), \qquad c\in\cO_C(U_C). \end{equation*}
Their images in \(\cO_D(U_D)\) agree after restriction to every \((U_i)_D\text{.}\) Since the structure presheaf for \(D\) is separated, they agree globally. Exactness on \(U\) therefore gives \(x\in\cO_R(U)\) mapping to \((b,c)\text{.}\) Exactness on each \(U_i\) shows that \(x|_{U_i}=x_i\text{,}\) and uniqueness follows in the same way. Thus \(r_R\) identifies \(\cO_R(U)\) homeomorphically with the matching-family equalizer, which is closed in the finite product because all section rings are Hausdorff.
🔗
The underlying presheaf is therefore a sheaf by the sheaf-on-a-basis theorem [bib-Stacks, Tag 009O]. To retain the topology on arbitrary opens, let \(V=\bigcup_\lambda V_\lambda\) be an open covering and let \(M\) be its matching-family equalizer with the subspace topology. For every rational \(U\subset V\text{,}\) quasi-compactness gives a finite rational cover of \(U\) subordinate to the \(V_\lambda\text{.}\) Restriction from \(M\) to that finite cover, followed by the rational-basis result, gives a continuous map \(M\to\cO_R(U)\text{,}\) independent of the chosen subordinate cover. These maps are compatible under rational restriction. Since
\begin{equation*} \cO_R(V)=\varprojlim_{U\subset V,\ U\ \mathrm{rational}}\cO_R(U) \end{equation*}
with its projective-limit topology, the inverse of the algebraic gluing map \(\cO_R(V)\to M\) is continuous. Hence the gluing map is a homeomorphism, as required.
