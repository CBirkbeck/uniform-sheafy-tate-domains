Proposition 7.5.

There is a canonical isometric isomorphism
\begin{equation} \mathcal A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal B, \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X.\tag{7.14} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The datum \((W;\varpi)\) already lies in the zeroth head \(H_0=k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Let
\begin{equation*} Q= H_0\langle T\rangle/ \overline{(\varpi T-W)} \end{equation*}
be its separated completed quotient. Evaluation at \(W=\varpi X\) and \(T=X\) gives a continuous map \(Q\to k\langle X\rangle\text{.}\) Conversely, evaluation of \(k\langle X\rangle\) at the image of \(T\) gives a continuous map in the other direction. The two maps are inverse by polynomial density, and the norm estimates in the two universal properties show that the isomorphism is isometric.
🔗
Apply the coefficientwise-localisation construction of Proposition 7.8 to this zeroth head. It identifies the rational chart with the \(c_0\) tail algebra over \(Q\text{.}\) After replacing \(Q\) by \(k\langle X\rangle\text{,}\) its elements and norm are exactly those in (7.12) and (7.13): the tail basis element of parity \(\nu\) contributes the factor \(\varpi^{\omega(\nu)}\text{.}\) This gives (7.14), and the head evaluation sends \(W\) to \(\varpi X\text{.}\)
