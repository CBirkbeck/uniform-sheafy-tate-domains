Proposition 7.8.

There is a bicontinuous ring isomorphism
\begin{equation} \mathcal A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal B, \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X.\tag{7.18} \end{equation}

Proof.
The datum \((W;\varpi)\) lies in \(\mathcal A^{(0)}=k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Let
\begin{equation*} P= \mathcal A^{(0)}\langle T\rangle/ \overline{(\varpi T-W)} \end{equation*}
be its separated completed quotient. Evaluation at \(W=\varpi X\) and \(T=X\) gives a continuous map \(P\to k\langle X\rangle\text{.}\) Conversely, evaluation of \(k\langle X\rangle\) at the image of \(T\) gives a continuous map in the other direction. The two maps are inverse by polynomial density, and the norm estimates in the two universal properties show that the isomorphism is isometric.
🔗
Apply Proposition 7.6 with \(N=0\text{.}\) It describes the rational chart as the ring of sums
\begin{equation*} \sum_\nu p_\nu e_\nu, \qquad p_\nu\in P,\qquad \lVert p_\nu\rVert\longrightarrow0. \end{equation*}
Under \(P\cong k\langle X\rangle\text{,}\) this is precisely the model (7.16). Its sup norm is (7.17), because the term indexed by \(\nu\) is multiplied by \(\varpi^{\omega(\nu)}\text{.}\) The map from \(\mathcal A^{(0)}\) sends \(W\) to \(\varpi X\text{.}\)
