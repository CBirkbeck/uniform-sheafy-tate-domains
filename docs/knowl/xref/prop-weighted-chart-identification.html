Proposition 7.6.

There is a canonical isometric isomorphism
\begin{equation} \mathcal A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal B, \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X.\tag{7.14} \end{equation}
The graph ideal \((W-\varpi X)\) in \(\mathcal A\langle X\rangle\) is closed, so no additional separated quotient is required.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
On rings of definition define
\begin{equation*} q_0:\mathcal A_0\langle X\rangle\longrightarrow\mathcal B_0, \qquad q_0(W^aX^cU^\nu)=\varpi^aX^{a+c}U^\nu. \end{equation*}
For a target monomial define a \(k^\circ\)-linear section by
\begin{equation} s\bigl(b_{d,\nu}X^dU^\nu\bigr) =\varpi^{-\omega(\nu)}b_{d,\nu} W^{\omega(\nu)}X^{d-\omega(\nu)}U^\nu.\tag{7.15} \end{equation}
The divisibility and support conditions in (7.12) make this well-defined, and (7.13) shows that \(s\) is isometric. Moreover, \(q_0s=\operatorname{id}\text{.}\)
🔗
It remains to identify the kernel. Let \(M=W^aX^cU^\nu\) and put \(r=a-\omega(\nu)\geq0\text{.}\) If \(r>0\text{,}\) set
\begin{equation*} D(M)=W^{\omega(\nu)}X^cU^\nu \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}W^{r-1-j}(\varpi X)^j, \end{equation*}
and set \(D(M)=0\) when \(r=0\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation} M-sq_0(M)=(W-\varpi X)D(M).\tag{7.16} \end{equation}
Each basis monomial occurring in \(D(M)\) is still allowed in \(\mathcal A_0\langle X\rangle\text{,}\) and all its scalar coefficients have norm at most one. Thus \(D\) is norm-nonincreasing on finite-support series and extends continuously to the \(c_0\)-completion. If \(x\in\ker(q_0)\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} x=x-sq_0(x)=(W-\varpi X)D(x). \end{equation*}
The reverse inclusion is immediate, so
\begin{equation} \ker(q_0)=(W-\varpi X)\mathcal A_0\langle X\rangle.\tag{7.17} \end{equation}
Since \(q_0\) is a split surjection onto the complete ring \(\mathcal B_0\text{,}\) this kernel is closed. The quotient norm is exactly (7.13): boundedness of \(q_0\) gives one inequality, and the isometric section gives the reverse inequality. Inverting \(\varpi\) proves (7.14) and the closedness assertion.
