Theorem 6.1. Milnor descent.

Let \(R,B,C,D\) be the underlying rings of complete nonarchimedean Huber pairs, with continuous maps from \(R\) to \(B\text{,}\) \(C\text{,}\) and \(D\text{,}\) and from \(B\) and \(C\) to \(D\text{.}\) Suppose that rational-localisation data on \(R\) can be carried functorially to the other three vertices, that the resulting maps on section rings commute, and that this construction transports rational covers, is natural under refinement, and carries matching families to compatible families on common refinements. Suppose also that for every rational-localisation datum \(U\) the map
\begin{equation*} \mathcal O_R(U)\longrightarrow \mathcal O_B(U_B)\oplus\mathcal O_C(U_C) \end{equation*}
is a topological embedding whose image is the equaliser of the two maps to \(\mathcal O_D(U_D)\text{,}\) compatibly with restriction and matching families. If \(B,C,D\) are sheafy, then \(R\) is sheafy.

Proof.
Push a finite rational cover of \(U\) to the other vertices. Separation over \(B\) and \(C\text{,}\) followed by injectivity of the map in the theorem, gives separation over \(R\text{.}\) A matching family glues over \(B\) and \(C\text{;}\) the two sections have the same image over \(D\text{,}\) and the equaliser hypothesis gives the unique section over \(R\text{.}\)
🔗
For the topology, the restriction map for \(R\) factors, through the topological embedding in the theorem, into the product of the restriction maps for \(B\) and \(C\text{.}\) Those maps are topological embeddings because \(B\) and \(C\) are sheafy. The factorisation therefore shows that the restriction map for \(R\) is also a topological embedding. This is the topological sheaf condition on finite rational covers; the rational-basis theorem gives the sheaf condition on all opens [bib-Stacks, Tag 009O].
