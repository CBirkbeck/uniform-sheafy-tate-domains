Proposition 7.6.

The ring \(\mathcal B\) is an integral domain but is not uniform.
🔗
Lean (chart is a domain)Lean (chart is nonuniform)
🔗

Proof.
Group a series in \(\mathcal B\) by its \(U\)-multi-index. This gives a coefficientwise homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \mathcal B\longrightarrow k\langle X\rangle[[U_1,U_2,\ldots]]. \end{equation*}
It is injective, and the target is a domain because \(k\langle X\rangle\) is a domain. Hence \(\mathcal B\) is a domain. In the finite-head formalisation below, this is the embedding of the corresponding \(c_0\) tail algebra into formal multivariable power series; the twisting element is \(\varpi X\text{,}\) which is a non-zero-divisor.
🔗
Inside \(\mathcal A\) write \(Y_n=W^nU_n\) and put
\begin{equation*} T_n=\varpi^{-n}Y_n=X^nU_n\in\mathcal B. \end{equation*}
Formula (7.10) gives
\begin{equation*} \lVert T_n\rVert_{\mathrm{in}}=|\varpi|^{-n}, \qquad \lVert T_n^2\rVert_{\mathrm{in}}=1. \end{equation*}
It follows by separating even and odd powers that the powers of each fixed \(T_n\) are bounded. Thus every \(T_n\) is power-bounded. The family \((T_n)_{n\geq1}\) is not bounded, since its norms \(|\varpi|^{-n}\) are unbounded. Hence \(\mathcal B^\circ\) is unbounded and \(\mathcal B\) is not uniform.
