Proposition 8.9.

The ring \(\mathcal B\) is an integral domain but is not uniform.

Proof.
The coefficient description gives a homomorphism
\begin{equation*} \mathcal B\longrightarrow k\langle X\rangle[[U_1,U_2,\ldots]], \qquad \sum_\nu p_\nu e_\nu\longmapsto \sum_\nu(\varpi X)^{\omega(\nu)}p_\nu(X)U^\nu. \end{equation*}
It is a homomorphism by (8.9). If its image is zero, then
\begin{equation*} (\varpi X)^{\omega(\nu)}p_\nu(X)=0 \end{equation*}
for every \(\nu\text{.}\) Since \(k\langle X\rangle\) is a domain, every \(p_\nu\) is zero. The map is therefore injective. Its target is a domain, and hence so is \(\mathcal B\text{.}\)

Inside \(\mathcal A\) write \(Y_n=W^nU_n\) and put
\begin{equation*} T_n=\varpi^{-n}Y_n=X^nU_n\in\mathcal B. \end{equation*}
Formula (8.11) gives
\begin{equation*} \lVert T_n\rVert_{\mathcal B}=|\varpi|^{-n}, \qquad \lVert T_n^2\rVert_{\mathcal B}=1. \end{equation*}
The even powers of \(T_n\) have norm at most \(1\text{,}\) and the odd powers have norm at most \(\lVert T_n\rVert_{\mathcal B}\text{.}\) Thus every \(T_n\) is power-bounded. The family \((T_n)_{n\geq1}\) is not bounded, since its norms \(|\varpi|^{-n}\) are unbounded. Hence \(\mathcal B^\circ\) is unbounded and \(\mathcal B\) is not uniform.
