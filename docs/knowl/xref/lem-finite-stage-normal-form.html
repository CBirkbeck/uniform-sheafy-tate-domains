Lemma 7.3. Isometric finite-stage normal form.

There is an isometric decomposition
\begin{equation} \mathcal A_N\cong \bigoplus_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^N}^{\max} k\langle W,Z_1,\ldots,Z_N\rangle Y^\epsilon.\tag{7.8} \end{equation}
Under the substitution
\begin{equation} Y_n\longmapsto W^nU_n, \qquad Z_n\longmapsto U_n^2,\tag{7.9} \end{equation}
this identifies \(\mathcal A_N\) isometrically with the closed subalgebra of \(k\langle W,U_1,\ldots,U_N\rangle\) supported on the monomials in (7.2) involving only \(U_1,\ldots,U_N\text{.}\) The transition maps \(\mathcal A_N\to\mathcal A_{N+1}\) are isometric, and
\begin{equation} \mathcal A=\widehat{\bigcup_{N\geq1}\mathcal A_N}.\tag{7.10} \end{equation}

🔗

Proof.
Successive division by the monic relations \(Y_n^2-W^{2n}Z_n\) reduces every class uniquely to a sum
\begin{equation*} \sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^N}f_\epsilon(W,Z_1,\ldots,Z_N)Y^\epsilon. \end{equation*}
The division process does not increase the Gauss norm, so the quotient norm is at most the maximum of the norms of the \(f_\epsilon\text{.}\) Under (7.9), the summand indexed by \(\epsilon\) has \(U_n\)-exponent congruent to \(\epsilon_n\) modulo \(2\text{.}\) Different parity classes therefore have disjoint monomial supports. The image of the normal form consequently has Gauss norm exactly \(\max_\epsilon\lVert f_\epsilon\rVert\text{.}\) Since the substitution is norm-nonincreasing on the original Tate algebra, the norm of this image is at most the quotient norm. This proves both uniqueness and the isometry (7.8).
🔗
Writing \(\nu_n=2q_n+\epsilon_n\) gives the unique factorization
\begin{equation} W^aU^\nu =W^{a-\omega(\nu)} \prod_{n=1}^NY_n^{\epsilon_n}Z_n^{q_n}\tag{7.11} \end{equation}
whenever \(a\geq\omega(\nu)\) and \(\nu\) is supported in \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\text{.}\) Thus the finite-stage image is exactly the claimed support algebra. The same description proves that the transition maps are isometric. Finally, every finite set of allowed monomials is contained in one finite stage, while finite-support series are dense in the restricted series (7.5); this proves (7.10).
