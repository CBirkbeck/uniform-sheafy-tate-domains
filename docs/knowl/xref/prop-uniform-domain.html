Proposition 3.1.

The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform nonnoetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain. Moreover,
\begin{equation*} A^\circ=A_0. \end{equation*}

Proof.
For \(0\neq c\in C\text{,}\) define
\begin{equation*} \nu(c)=\max\{n\in\mathbf Z:c\in\varpi^nC_0\}. \end{equation*}
This maximum exists because \(C_0\) is \(\varpi\)-adically separated and \(C=C_0[1/\varpi]\text{.}\) We claim that
\begin{equation} \nu(cc')=\nu(c)+\nu(c') \qquad (c,c'\neq 0).\tag{3.5} \end{equation}
After multiplying \(c\) and \(c'\) by suitable powers of \(\varpi\text{,}\) it is enough to treat the case \(c,c'\in C_0\setminus\varpi C_0\text{.}\) Their reductions are nonzero elements of
\begin{equation*} C_0/\varpi C_0\cong\widetilde{k}[W,W^{-1},Q]. \end{equation*}
This is a domain, so the product of the reductions is nonzero. This proves (3.5); in particular, \(C\) is a domain.
🔗
By the preceding coefficient description, \(A\) consists of the same restricted series as \(A_0\text{,}\) but with coefficients in \(k\text{.}\) Such a series lies in \(C_0\) precisely when all of its coefficients lie in \(k^\circ\text{.}\) Hence
\begin{equation*} A_0=A\cap C_0. \end{equation*}
Every element of \(A_0\) is power-bounded because \(A_0\) is a subring. Conversely, let \(a\in A\setminus A_0\text{.}\) Then \(\nu(a)<0\text{,}\) and by (3.5), \(\nu(a^n)=n\nu(a)\text{.}\) Hence the powers of \(a\) cannot all lie in any fixed \(\varpi^{-r}A_0\text{.}\) Thus \(a\) is not power-bounded. It follows that \(A^\circ=A_0\text{,}\) so \(A\) is uniform. Completeness follows from the completeness of \(A_0\text{,}\) and \(A\) is a domain because it is a subring of \(C\text{.}\)
🔗
For nonnoetherianity, let \(J=\ker(\iota_B)=Q^2C\subset A\text{.}\) If \(J\) were generated by \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\text{,}\) then, for every \(\ell\in L\text{,}\) comparison of the \(Q^2\)-coefficient in an expression
\begin{equation*} Q^2\ell=\sum_i x_i a_i,\qquad x_i\in A, \end{equation*}
would express \(\ell\) as a \(k\langle W\rangle\)-linear combination of the \(Q^2\)-coefficients of the \(a_i\text{.}\) This would make \(L=k\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) a finite \(k\langle W\rangle\)-module, which is impossible: a finite algebra is integral, whereas a monic equation for \(W^{-1}\text{,}\) after multiplication by a power of \(W\text{,}\) would put \(1\) in the proper ideal \((W)\subset k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Thus \(J\) is not finitely generated, and \(A\) is not noetherian.
