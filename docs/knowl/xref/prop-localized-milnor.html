Proposition 5.3.

For every unit-ideal datum \(\alpha\) as above, the canonical sequence of graph quotients
\begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow A_\alpha\longrightarrow B_\alpha\oplus C_\alpha\longrightarrow D_\alpha\longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}
is strict exact, and \(C_\alpha\to D_\alpha\) is a strict surjection. Equivalently,
\begin{equation*} A_\alpha\cong B_\alpha\times_{D_\alpha}C_\alpha. \end{equation*}

Proof.
The ideals \(I_B,I_C,I_D\) are closed by Lemma 5.1, and \(I_A\) is closed by Lemma 5.2. Thus
\begin{equation*} E_\alpha=T_E/I_E \qquad(E=A,B,C,D). \end{equation*}
🔗
We first prove algebraic exactness. If a representative \(p\in T_A\) maps into both \(I_B\) and \(I_C\text{,}\) the pullback assertion in Lemma 5.2 gives an element of \(I_A\) with the same two images. Injectivity of \(T_A\to T_B\oplus T_C\) then shows that \(p\in I_A\text{.}\) This gives injectivity on the left. Conversely, take compatible classes in \(T_B/I_B\) and \(T_C/I_C\) and choose representatives \(b\) and \(c\text{.}\) Their defect in \(T_D\) belongs to \(I_D\text{.}\) Lift this defect to an element of \(I_C\) using the strict surjection in (5.4), and add it to \(c\text{.}\) The corrected representatives agree in \(T_D\text{,}\) so (5.3) lifts them to \(T_A\text{.}\) This proves algebraic exactness.
🔗
For the topology on the left, let \(x\in A_\alpha\) and put
\begin{equation*} M=\max\{\lVert x_B\rVert,\lVert x_C\rVert\}. \end{equation*}
Choose representatives \(b\in T_B\) and \(c\in T_C\) whose norms are arbitrarily close to the quotient norms of \(x_B\) and \(x_C\text{.}\) Their defect lies in \(I_D\text{.}\) The norm estimate for \(I_C\to I_D\) proved in Lemma 5.2 gives a constant \(C_0\) and a correction \(y\in I_C\) mapping to this defect such that
\begin{equation*} \lVert y\rVert\leq C_0\lVert b_D-c_D\rVert. \end{equation*}
Thus \(b\) and \(c+y\) agree in \(T_D\) and lift to \(p\in T_A\text{.}\) The max-norm identity for the ambient pullback gives, after letting the error in the representatives tend to zero,
\begin{equation*} \lVert x\rVert\leq C M \end{equation*}
for a constant \(C\) depending only on the datum. The two quotient maps are norm-nonincreasing, so this estimate says precisely that \(A_\alpha\to B_\alpha\oplus C_\alpha\) is a topological embedding.
🔗
The coefficientwise section \(T_D\to T_C\) shows directly that \(C_\alpha\to D_\alpha\) is surjective. More precisely, choose a representative in \(T_D\) whose norm is arbitrarily close to the quotient norm of a given class in \(D_\alpha\text{,}\) and apply the norm-preserving section. Its class in \(C_\alpha\) has no larger norm. Thus the surjection is open; together with continuity, this says that it is strict.
