    Uniform sheafy Tate rings that are not stably uniform

    Christopher Birkbeck
    Alex Torzewski
    Abstract
    Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara show that a stably uniform Tate Huber ring is sheafy. We construct examples of uniform and sheafy Tate rings which are not stably uniform, answering Question 7 of Kedlaya’s Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. In the first example a rational localisation is non-reduced, whilst in the second the nonuniform rational localisation is an integral domain. The two main results are due to ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. Both constructions, including their sheafiness and the rational localisations witnessing failure of stable uniformity, have been formalised in Lean 4.
    🔗
    🔗

    Section 1 Introduction

    The examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara show that uniformity can be lost on a rational chart and, separately, that a uniform Tate Huber ring need not be sheafy [bib-BV], [bib-Mihara]. They leave open the possibility of a uniform sheafy ring which is not stably uniform. Hansen and Kedlaya ask this explicitly [bib-HK, Remark 3.16]; it is also Question 7 of Kedlaya’s Nonarchimedean Scottish Book [bib-Scottish]. The answer is no.
    🔗
    ChatGPT 5.6 Sol found both examples and supplied the proofs. The Lean formalisation was carried out by Claude Code. We have aimed to cite related work that may have informed this process; see Remark 1.3. Section 9 describes how we used ChatGPT in the search for these examples, and Appendix A records the formal definitions and theorem statements.
    🔗
    Our first example is the “finite-jet pullback”. This is a non-noetherian uniform integral domain for which a distinguished rational localisation is non-reduced, and hence is no longer uniform. Let \(k\) be a complete discretely valued nonarchimedean field, with valuation ring \(k^\circ\text{,}\) uniformizer \(\varpi\text{,}\) and residue field \(\widetilde{k}\text{.}\) Put
    \begin{equation*} L_0=k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle, \qquad B_0=k^\circ\langle W,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} C_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle, \qquad D_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    where \(k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) means \(k^\circ\langle W,V\rangle/(WV-1)\text{.}\) There are natural maps \(B_0\to D_0\) and \(C_0\onto D_0\text{.}\) Define
    \begin{equation*} A_0=B_0\times_{D_0}C_0, \qquad A=A_0[1/\varpi], \end{equation*}
    and endow \(A\) with the topology defined by the ring of definition \(A_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The main result is the following.
    🔗

    Theorem 1.1.

    The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform non-noetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain, with \(A^\circ=A_0\text{.}\) The Huber pair \((A,A^\circ)\) is sheafy. However,
    \begin{equation*} A\left\langle \frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \cong k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad X=\frac{W}{\varpi}, \end{equation*}
    so \(A\) is not stably uniform.

    🔗
    This construction also gives a new example solving Problems 24 and 28 of the Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. We recall the two problems and give the argument in Section 7. These results have also been formalised in Lean.
    🔗
    The proof uses Milnor squares of complete Tate rings. Recall that a cartesian square of rings with a surjective leg is called a Milnor square [bib-Milnor, Section 2]; the complete Tate setting is used by Kerz–Saito–Tamme in their work on analytic \(K\)-theory [bib-KST, Section 3].
    🔗

    Definition 1.2.

    A commutative square of complete Tate \(k\)-algebras
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} R&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    is a strict Milnor square if it is cartesian, the sequence of underlying topological \(k\)-vector spaces
    \begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow B\oplus C \xrightarrow{\ (b,c)\mapsto \bar b-\bar c\ }D\longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}
    is strict exact, and \(C\to D\) is a strict surjection.
    🔗
    🔗
    The maps \(C\to D\) and \(B\oplus C\to D\) are continuous surjections between complete normed \(k\)-vector spaces, and \(A\) has the subspace topology as the kernel of the second. The nonarchimedean open mapping theorem [bib-KST, Lemma 3.1] therefore shows that the square defining \(A\) is strict. Our main input is that, for this finite-jet square, the strict exact row persists after completed rational localisation and is compatible with refinement. This transfers sheafiness from \(B,C,D\) to \(A\text{.}\) The second example shows that the failure of stable uniformity need not come from a non-reduced localisation.

    🔗

    The weighted-parity construction of Section 8 is closer to the infinite monomial-support examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara. Its additional feature is that rational localisation can be calculated in a noetherian finite-variable subring.

    Section 2 Conventions and notation

    We follow Huber for Huber pairs, adic spectra, rational subsets, and rational localisation [bib-Huber94], and use Wedhorn’s account for strong noetherianity and sheafiness [bib-Wedhorn, Proposition and Definition 6.36, Section 8.1]; the bounded-denominator language for strictness follows Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV], and the quotient presentation of a rational localisation is also discussed by Hansen–Kedlaya [bib-HK].
    🔗
    • Tate topology. Throughout, \(E\) denotes a complete Tate \(k\)-algebra. In the discretely valued setting of this paper, this means that \(E\) contains a topologically nilpotent unit and admits an open bounded \(k^\circ\)-subalgebra \(E_0\) such that
      \begin{equation*} E=E_0[1/\varpi], \qquad \{\varpi^nE_0\}_{n\geq0} \end{equation*}
      is a basis of neighbourhoods of zero. Such an \(E_0\) is called a ring of definition. We always choose it closed, hence \(\varpi\)-adically complete. A subset of \(E\) is bounded if it is contained in \(\varpi^{-r}E_0\) for some \(r\geq0\text{.}\) If \(M\) is a complete topological \(k\)-vector space, an open bounded \(k^\circ\)-submodule \(M_0\subset M\) will be called a lattice.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Uniformity and noetherianity. We write \(E^\circ\) for the ring of power-bounded elements. The ring \(E\) is uniform if \(E^\circ\) is bounded, and strongly noetherian if \(E\langle Z_1,\ldots,Z_s\rangle\) is noetherian for every \(s\geq0\text{.}\) A Tate ring is stably uniform if every rational localisation is uniform.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Huber pairs and sheafiness. A ring of integral elements is an open subring \(E^+\subseteq E^\circ\) which is integrally closed in \(E\text{.}\) We call the Huber pair \((E,E^+)\) sheafy when its structure presheaf, valued in complete topological rings, satisfies the sheaf condition. Thus, for a finite rational cover \(U=\bigcup_iU_i\text{,}\) restriction identifies \(\mathcal O(U)\) homeomorphically with the subspace of \(\prod_i\mathcal O(U_i)\) consisting of tuples whose restrictions to every \(U_i\cap U_j\) agree.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Strictness. We use the following bounded-denominator form of strictness. Let \(u:M\to N\) be a continuous \(k\)-linear map and choose lattices \(M_0\subset M\) and \(N_0\subset N\text{.}\) Then \(u\) is strict if and only if there exists \(a\geq0\) such that
      \begin{equation} \varpi^a\bigl(u(M)\cap N_0\bigr)\subset u(M_0).\tag{2.1} \end{equation}
      For a surjection this becomes \(\varpi^aN_0\subset u(M_0)\text{.}\) For an injection it says that the lattice induced from the target is bounded in the source. This is the criterion used by Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV, Lemma 2]. We use the term strict Milnor square in the sense of Definition 1.2; the underlying algebraic terminology is standard [bib-Milnor], [bib-KST].
      🔗
      🔗
    • Rational localisation. We take every rational datum to have at least one numerator; an empty list may be padded by \(f_1=0\text{.}\) Let \(\alpha=(f_1,\dots,f_m;g)\) be a rational datum in \(E\text{,}\) so the ideal generated by \(g,f_1,\dots,f_m\) is open. Since \(E\) is a Tate \(k\)-algebra, this ideal is the unit ideal. After multiplying the entire datum by a common power of \(\varpi\text{,}\) we may assume that all entries lie in \(E_0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      The rational localisation \(E_\alpha\) is the \(\varpi\)-adic completion of \(E[1/g]\) for the ring of definition generated by
      \begin{equation*} E_0\left[\frac{f_1}{g},\dots,\frac{f_m}{g}\right]. \end{equation*}
      For such a ring of definition \(R\text{,}\) we use the notation
      \begin{equation*} \widehat R=\varprojlim_{n\geq1}R/\varpi^nR, \qquad \ker(R\longrightarrow\widehat R)=\bigcap_{n\geq1}\varpi^nR. \end{equation*}
      \(E_\alpha\) is independent of the ring of definition and of the presentation of the rational domain, and it is transitive under rational refinement. Put
      \begin{equation*} T_E=E\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle =E_0\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle[1/\varpi], \qquad r_i=gT_i-f_i, \end{equation*}
      and
      \begin{equation} I_{E,\alpha}:=(r_1,\dots,r_m) =\operatorname{im}\left( T_E^m\xrightarrow{\ d_{1,E}\ }T_E \right)\subset T_E, \qquad d_{1,E}(u_1,\dots,u_m)=\sum_i u_i(gT_i-f_i),\tag{2.2} \end{equation}
      Then
      \begin{equation} E_\alpha\cong T_E/\overline{I_{E,\alpha}}.\tag{2.3} \end{equation}
      Here the bar is necessary until the ideal has been shown to be closed. This is the standard quotient presentation of rational localisation; see [bib-Huber94, Notations, (1.2), Proposition 1.3, Lemma 1.5] and [bib-HK, Definition 3.6].

    Section 3 The finite-jet ring

    In this section we identify the finite-jet algebra with a concrete subring of \(C\) and prove that it is a complete uniform domain. Recall that
    \begin{equation*} L_0=k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle, \qquad B_0=k^\circ\langle W,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} C_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle, \qquad D_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    where \(k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) denotes \(k^\circ\langle W,V\rangle/(WV-1)\text{.}\) The natural maps \(B_0\to D_0\) and \(C_0\onto D_0\) define
    \begin{equation*} A_0=B_0\times_{D_0}C_0. \end{equation*}
    We write
    \begin{equation*} A=A_0[1/\varpi],\quad L=L_0[1/\varpi],\quad B=B_0[1/\varpi],\quad C=C_0[1/\varpi],\quad D=D_0[1/\varpi]. \end{equation*}
    Each of these Tate rings carries the topology defined by its subscript-zero ring.
    🔗
    The quotient map \(C_0\to D_0\) has a continuous \(k^\circ\)-linear section
    \begin{equation*} \sigma:D_0\longrightarrow C_0,\qquad \sigma(f_0+Qf_1)=f_0+Qf_1, \end{equation*}
    obtained by viewing the unique representative of \(Q\)-degree at most one as an element of \(C_0\text{.}\) Consequently
    \begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow A_0\longrightarrow B_0\oplus C_0 \longrightarrow D_0\longrightarrow 0\tag{3.1} \end{equation}
    is exact, and \(A_0\) is \(\varpi\)-adically complete. After inverting \(\varpi\) we obtain a strict Milnor square
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} A&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D. \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    In fact, the chosen rings of definition already make the integral row exact, so no powers of \(\varpi\) are lost in the defining square.
    🔗
    The map \(B_0\to D_0\) is injective. Projection to \(C_0\) therefore identifies \(A_0\) with the subring
    \begin{equation} A_0= \left\{ f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h(W,W^{-1},Q): f_0,f_1\in k^\circ\langle W\rangle, \ h\in C_0 \right\}.\tag{3.2} \end{equation}
    Equivalently, \(A_0\) consists of the restricted series
    \begin{equation} \sum_{(a,b)\in S}c_{a,b}W^aQ^b, \qquad S=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z\times\mathbb N: b\leq 1\Rightarrow a\geq 0\},\tag{3.3} \end{equation}
    with \(c_{a,b}\in k^\circ\text{.}\) Here restricted means that, for every \(n\text{,}\) only finitely many coefficients are nonzero modulo \(\varpi^n\text{.}\) The set \(S\) is closed under addition, so this is a subring of \(C_0\text{.}\) The same description with coefficients in \(k\) gives \(A\subset C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Equip \(A,B,C,D\) with the coefficientwise Gauss norms whose unit balls are the displayed rings of definition. Write
    \begin{equation*} \iota_B:A\to B,\qquad \iota_C:A\to C,\qquad \rho_B:B\to D,\qquad \rho_C:C\to D \end{equation*}
    for the structural maps, and give \(A\) the restricted Gauss norm from \(C\text{.}\) For \(a=f_0+Qf_1+Q^2h\text{,}\) the coefficient formulas give
    \begin{equation} \lVert\iota_C(a)\rVert_C=\lVert a\rVert_A, \qquad \lVert\iota_B(a)\rVert_B\leq \lVert a\rVert_A, \qquad \lVert\rho_B(b)\rVert_D\leq\lVert b\rVert_B, \qquad \lVert\rho_C(c)\rVert_D\leq\lVert c\rVert_C.\tag{3.4} \end{equation}
    Thus every structural map is norm-nonincreasing.
    🔗

    Proposition 3.1.

    The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform non-noetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain. Moreover,
    \begin{equation*} A^\circ=A_0. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    The Gauss norm on \(L=k\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) is multiplicative, and the restricted Gauss norm on \(C=L\langle Q\rangle\) is therefore multiplicative as well. In particular, \(C\) is a domain, and hence so is its subring \(A\text{.}\) The conditions on the constant and linear \(Q\)-coefficients which define \(A\) are closed conditions in \(C\text{,}\) so \(A\) is complete. The image of \(\varpi\) in \(A\) is a topologically nilpotent unit, and therefore \(A\) is a Tate ring.
    🔗
    The coefficient description of \(A_0\) shows that it is exactly the unit ball for the restricted Gauss norm on \(A\text{.}\) Since this norm is multiplicative,
    \begin{equation*} \lVert a^n\rVert=\lVert a\rVert^n. \end{equation*}
    It follows that \(a\) is power-bounded if and only if \(\lVert a\rVert\leq1\text{.}\) Thus \(A^\circ=A_0\text{,}\) and the unit ball is bounded, so \(A\) is uniform.
    🔗
    To see that \(A\) is not noetherian, let \(J=\ker(\iota_B)=Q^2C\subset A\text{.}\) If \(J\) were generated by \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\text{,}\) write \(b_i\in L\) for the coefficient of \(Q^2\) in \(a_i\text{.}\) For every \(\ell\in L\) we could write
    \begin{equation*} Q^2\ell=\sum_i x_i a_i,\qquad x_i\in A, \end{equation*}
    and write \(c_i\) for the constant coefficient of \(x_i\) as a series in \(Q\text{.}\) By (3.2), we have \(c_i\in k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Since \(a_i\in Q^2C\text{,}\) its coefficients in degrees \(0\) and \(1\) vanish. The coefficient of \(Q^2\) in \(x_i a_i\) is therefore exactly \(c_i b_i\text{.}\) Taking the coefficient of \(Q^2\) in the displayed equality gives
    \begin{equation*} \ell=\sum_i c_i b_i,\qquad c_i\in k\langle W\rangle. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) would generate \(L\) as a \(k\langle W\rangle\)-module. This is impossible. Indeed, it would make \(W^{-1}\) integral over \(k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Multiplying a monic relation for \(W^{-1}\) of degree \(n\) by \(W^n\) would put \(1\) in the proper ideal \((W)\subset k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Thus \(J\) is not finitely generated, and \(A\) is not noetherian.

    Section 4 A nonuniform rational localisation

    We next compute the rational chart which witnesses the failure of stable uniformity.
    🔗
    Consider the datum
    \begin{equation*} \alpha=(W;\varpi). \end{equation*}
    The ideal \((\varpi,W)\) is open, since \(\varpi\) is a unit in \(A\text{.}\) Let
    \begin{equation*} A_\alpha=A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \end{equation*}
    be the corresponding rational localisation on \(|W|\leq |\varpi|\text{,}\) and write \(X=W/\varpi\text{.}\) Since \(\varpi\) is already invertible in \(A\text{,}\) the algebraic localisation does not change the ring. More explicitly, \(A_\alpha\) is obtained by taking the \(\varpi\)-adic completion of \(A_0[X]\subset A\) and then inverting \(\varpi\text{.}\)

    Section 5 Localisation of the Milnor square

    In this section we prove that rational localisation preserves the defining pullback. We first record the strict Koszul estimate needed for the proof.
    🔗

    Subsection 5.1 Koszul complexes for rational localisations

    Let \(E\) be one of \(B,C,D\text{,}\) with its coefficientwise Gauss norm and the displayed unit ball \(E_0\text{.}\) Let \(f_1,\dots,f_m,g\in E_0\) generate the unit ideal in \(E\text{.}\) Put
    \begin{equation*} T_E=E\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle, \qquad T_{E,0}=E_0\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle, \end{equation*}
    and set \(r_i=gT_i-f_i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    We give \(T_E\) the topology for which \(\{\varpi^nT_{E,0}\}_{n\geq0}\) is a basis of neighbourhoods of zero.     \begin{equation*} W^aU^\nu =W^{a-\omega(\nu)} \prod_{n=1}^NZ_n^{q_n} \prod_{n=1}^NY_n^{\epsilon_n}. \end{equation*}
    Grouping the monomials with the same tuple \((\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_N)\) gives (8.7). The finitely many \(Y^\epsilon\) therefore generate \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) over \(\mathcal T^{(N)}\text{.}\) The same argument after adjoining any finite number of Tate variables gives strong noetherianity. The inclusions are coefficientwise and hence isometric. Finally, approximate \(f\) to the required precision by a finite sum of monomials and choose \(N\) so that only \(U_1,\ldots,U_N\) occur in that sum. This gives the stated estimate and (8.8).

    Theorem 8.10.

    Proof.

    with coefficientwise restriction maps. Here \(P\) is a rational localisation of the strongly noetherian Tate ring \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\text{,}\) and the \(P_i\) are rational localisations corresponding to the \(U_i\text{.}\) Let \(V\) and \(V_i\) be the rational subsets of
    \begin{equation*} \Spa\bigl(\mathcal A^{(N)},(\mathcal A^{(N)})^\circ\bigr) \end{equation*}
    defined by the data giving \(P\) and \(P_i\text{.}\) Then \(V=\bigcup_iV_i\text{.}\) Indeed, if \(v\in V\text{,}\) the composite \(v\circ\rho_N\) is a point of \(\Spa(\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\text{,}\) since \(\rho_N\) sends power-bounded elements to power-bounded elements. For data in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\text{,}\) membership in the corresponding rational subset is unchanged by this pullback because \(\rho_N\) is a retraction. Thus \(v\circ\rho_N\) belongs to \(U\text{,}\) and hence to some \(U_i\text{,}\) which gives \(v\in V_i\text{.}\) We may therefore apply sheafiness of the strongly noetherian ring \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) to the cover \(V=\bigcup_iV_i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Let \((x_i)_i\) be a matching family and write \(x_i=\sum_\mu x_{i,\mu}e_\mu\text{.}\) Since \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) is sheafy, the matching family \((x_{i,\mu})_i\) glues to a unique \(q_\mu\in P\) for every \(\mu\text{.}\) It remains to check that \(\lVert q_\mu\rVert\to0\text{.}\) The restrictions of the \(q_\mu\) to every \(P_i\) tend to zero because the coefficients of each \(x_i\) do. The product restriction map
    \begin{equation*} P\longrightarrow\prod_iP_i \end{equation*}
    is a topological embedding, so it reflects convergence to zero. Hence \(q_\mu\to0\text{,}\) and \(\sum_\mu q_\mu e_\mu\) is the required section over \(U\text{.}\) The same coefficientwise argument proves separation.
    🔗
    The image of the product restriction map for \(\mathcal A\) is therefore the subspace of tuples whose restrictions to every pairwise intersection agree. This subspace is closed: it is the common zero locus of the continuous difference maps. The restriction map is continuous and injective. The nonarchimedean closed-range theorem over the Tate ring \(\mathcal A\) shows that its inverse on this image is continuous, so restriction is a topological embedding. We have proved the topological sheaf condition on finite rational covers. The general rational-basis theorem upgrades this to the all-open structure presheaf, and its plus-ring independence gives the same conclusion for every ring of integral elements. In particular, \((\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\) is sheafy.

    Remark 8.11. Admissible blow-ups.

    There is a common formal interpretation of the two bad rational charts. Let \(R_0\) denote \(A_0\) in the finite-jet example and \(\mathcal A_0\) in the weighted-parity example. The affine chart of the admissible blow-up of \(\operatorname{Spf}(R_0)\) along \((\varpi,W)\) on which the pullback of this ideal is generated by \(\varpi\) has ring
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{R_0[W/\varpi]}. \end{equation*}
    Here \(R_0[W/\varpi]\) means the subring of \(R_0[1/\varpi]\) generated by \(R_0\) and \(W/\varpi\text{,}\) and the hat denotes the \(\varpi\)-adic completion defined above. After inverting \(\varpi\text{,}\) its coordinate ring is the completed rational localisation \(R_0[1/\varpi]\langle W/\varpi\rangle\text{,}\) corresponding to \(|W|\leq|\varpi|\text{.}\) This description applies to our non-noetherian formal models because they are \(\varpi\)-adic and their ideal of definition \((\varpi)\) is principal; see [bib-FK, Chapter 0, Section 7.1(c), and Chapter II, Section 1.1(b)].
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} Q^2\in\bigcap_{n\geq1}\varpi^nA_0[W/\varpi]. \end{equation*}
    This intersection is the kernel of the completion map. Thus \(Q^2\) is killed, while \(Q\) survives, so this rational chart is non-reduced. The map from \(A\) to this completed rational localisation is also not flat by Proposition 7.1. For \(R_0=\mathcal A_0\text{,}\) the completed chart remains a domain, but its generic fibre is nonuniform because the power-bounded elements \(T_n=X^nU_n\) form an unbounded family. Thus the two examples give different failures on the same kind of affine blow-up chart.

The infinite monomial support and the unbounded power-bounded elements are in the tradition of the examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara; the approximation by the rings \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) is what makes sheafiness accessible here.

    Section 9 How the examples were found

    The authors had for some time been interested in finding a counterexample to Problem 7 of the Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. Our attempts had centred on the example of [bib-BV, Section 4.5, Proposition 17], which is uniform but not stably uniform. We believe that this example is in fact sheafy, but we have not found a proof.
    🔗
    In January 2026 we began to experiment with AI tools, initially ChatGPT 5.2 Pro. Buzzard–Verberkmoes reduce exactness for a two-member Laurent cover to a boundedness condition on the relevant rings of definition [bib-BV, Lemma 2]. For a uniform ring this follows from boundedness of the power-bounded elements [bib-BV, Lemma 3 and Corollary 4]. Their reduction from general rational covers to Laurent covers is given in [bib-BV, Lemma 8 and the proof of Theorem 7]. For sheafiness these checks must also be made after rational localisation. Their Theorem 7 cannot be applied directly to Proposition 17, since one of the rational localisations in that example is not uniform. Our plan was instead to ask ChatGPT to write down many explicit rational covers and check the required exactness directly for each one, in the hope that a pattern, and eventually a proof, would emerge.
    🔗
    After a couple of weeks ChatGPT 5.2 Pro claimed to have a complete proof. The authors then began the slow process of understanding this proof, only to discover, with the help of ChatGPT 5.3, that one of its arguments used the following assertion:
    🔗
    If \(\phi:A\to B\) is a continuous homomorphism of Hausdorff topological abelian groups and \(A\) is complete, then \(\phi(A)\) is closed in \(B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This assertion is false, and its use completely broke the purported proof.
    🔗
    Motivated by this, we began in parallel to use Claude Code to formalise the parts of the theory of adic spaces needed here, including the theorem that strongly noetherian Tate rings are sheafy. The aim was a practical one: future AI-generated proofs could be formalised before the human authors had spent days understanding an argument containing a fatal error near its end.
    🔗
    We then returned to writing down covers and looking for a pattern. This continued through ChatGPT 5.3 and subsequent versions. When ChatGPT 5.6 Sol became available, we asked, almost in passing, whether it could prove the result we wanted or find a new example. It produced the examples studied here. By then the Lean development was far enough advanced that we could use Claude Code to formalise the arguments quickly in Lean and check that they were correct.

If \(W\subseteq V\text{,}\) then limitRestrict is the map \(\mathcal O_X(V)\to\mathcal O_X(W)\) obtained by reindexing this family. The corresponding all-open condition is IsLimitSheaf, from SheafyPair.lean.
    🔗
    The class HasLocLiftPowerBounded is used internally to construct the restriction maps between completed rational localisations. For an inclusion \(R(D')\subseteq R(D)\text{,}\) it says that the denominator of \(D\) becomes a unit on \(R(D')\) and that the fractions \(t/s\text{,}\) for \(t\) in the numerator set of \(D\text{,}\) are power-bounded there. This class is supplied automatically for the complete Tate pairs considered here; it is not an additional hypothesis in our results.
    🔗

    Subsection A.6 Mathematical and code provenance

    The formal structure presheaf, rational-cover criterion, and the strongly-noetherian sheaf theorem follow Huber’s construction [bib-Huber94, Theorem 2.2 and Lemmas 2.3--2.6] and Wedhorn’s systematic formulation [bib-Wedhorn, Proposition and Definition 6.36, Remark 8.20, Definition 8.26, Theorem 8.28(b), Corollary 8.32, and Lemmas 8.33--8.34]. The passage between a fixed plus ring and all plus rings follows the standard rational-refinement argument recorded by Kedlaya [bib-KedlayaAWS, Lemma 1.6.8 and Remark 1.6.9]. Bambozzi–Kremnizer formulate the general construction in terms of Koszul complexes and prove strict Koszul regularity [bib-BK, Notation 4.3, Definitions 4.5--4.6, and Corollary 4.13]. The formalisation does not invoke this result. Positive-degree exactness is instead proved by flat base change from the polynomial sequence; closedness and the nonarchimedean open mapping theorem [bib-KST, Lemma 3.1] give constants bounding the norm of a preimage by a fixed multiple of the norm of its image. Multiplication by a suitable power of \(\varpi\) then yields the bounded-denominator criterion used by Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV, Lemma 2].
    🔗
    The development is written in Lean 4 [bib-Lean4] on top of mathlib [bib-mathlib]. The formalisation-specific files live in AINTLIB [bib-AINTLIB]. AINTLIB includes code adapted from William Coram’s restricted-power-series development [bib-CoramCode]; its univariate Gauss-norm layer builds on Fabrizio Barroero’s mathlib code [bib-BarroeroCode]. It separately uses Bingyu Xia’s multivariable power-series equivalences [bib-XiaCode]. The vendored source headers and commit history retain the respective attributions. The finite-jet and weighted-parity definitions, localisation proofs, and sheaf endpoints discussed in this appendix are the AINTLIB-specific part of the development.
