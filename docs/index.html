    Uniform sheafy Tate rings that are not stably uniform

    Christopher Birkbeck
    Alex Torzewski
    Abstract
    Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara show that a stably uniform Tate Huber ring is sheafy. We construct examples of uniform and strongly sheafy Tate rings which are not stably uniform, answering Question 7 of Kedlaya’s Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. In the first example a rational localisation is non-reduced, whilst in the second the rational localisation is an integral domain but is not uniform. The two main results are due to ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. Both constructions, the rational localisations witnessing failure of stable uniformity, and their strong sheafiness have been formalised in Lean 4.
    🔗
    🔗

    Section 1 Introduction

    Work of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and, separately, Mihara shows that every stably uniform Tate Huber ring is sheafy, but leaves open whether every uniform sheafy Tate Huber ring is stably uniform [bib-BV], [bib-Mihara]. Hansen and Kedlaya ask this explicitly [bib-HK, Remark 3.16]; it is also Question 7 of Kedlaya’s Nonarchimedean Scottish Book [bib-Scottish]. We answer this question by constructing uniform and sheafy Tate rings that are not stably uniform. In fact, we prove the stronger statement that both examples are strongly sheafy. Both are non-noetherian, so neither Huber’s theorem for strongly noetherian Tate rings nor the stably uniform criterion applies.
    🔗
    The examples and proofs described here were found with the help of AI agents, specifically ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. In Section 9 we describe how we used ChatGPT in the search for these examples. Every result discussed here has also been fully formalised in Lean by Claude Code, and Appendix A records the formal definitions and theorem statements. This article was prepared with the help of AI from an initial human-written skeleton. We have kept many of the names given to our objects by the AI systems. This is a conscious choice intended to make it easier to trace sources that may have influenced their work; see Remark 1.3.
    🔗
    We start with the “finite-jet pullback” example. This is a non-noetherian uniform integral domain for which a certain rational localisation is non-reduced, and hence is no longer uniform. Let \(k\) be a complete discretely valued nonarchimedean field, with valuation ring \(k^\circ\text{,}\) uniformizer \(\varpi\text{,}\) and residue field \(\widetilde{k}\text{.}\) Put
    \begin{equation*} L_0=k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle, \qquad B_0=k^\circ\langle W,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} C_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle, \qquad D_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    where \(k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) means \(k^\circ\langle W,V\rangle/(WV-1)\text{.}\) There are natural maps \(B_0\to D_0\) and \(C_0\onto D_0\text{.}\) Define
    \begin{equation*} A_0=B_0\times_{D_0}C_0, \qquad A=A_0[1/\varpi], \end{equation*}
    and endow \(A\) with the topology defined by the ring of definition \(A_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The main result is the following.
    🔗

    Theorem 1.1.

    The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform non-noetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain, with \(A^\circ=A_0\text{.}\) It is strongly sheafy; in particular, the Huber pair \((A,A^\circ)\) is sheafy. However,
    \begin{equation*} A\left\langle \frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \cong k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad X=\frac{W}{\varpi}, \end{equation*}
    so \(A\) is not stably uniform.

    This construction also gives a new example solving Problems 24 and 28 of the Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. We recall the two problems and give the argument in Section 7.
    🔗
    The key element in this example is that after the rational localisation \(A\to A\langle W/\varpi\rangle\text{,}\) \(Q^2\) vanishes whereas \(Q\) does not.
    🔗
    The proof uses Milnor squares of complete Tate rings. Recall that a cartesian square of rings with a surjective leg is called a Milnor square [bib-Milnor, Section 2]; the complete Tate setting is used by Kerz–Saito–Tamme in their work on analytic \(K\)-theory [bib-KST, Section 3].
    🔗

    Definition 1.2.

    A commutative square of complete Tate \(k\)-algebras
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} A&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    is a strict Milnor square if it is cartesian, the sequence of underlying topological \(k\)-vector spaces
    \begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow B\oplus C \xrightarrow{\ (b,c)\mapsto \bar b-\bar c\ }D\longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}
    is strict exact, and \(C\to D\) is a strict surjection.
    🔗
    🔗
    The maps \(C\to D\) and \(B\oplus C\to D\) are continuous surjections between complete normed \(k\)-vector spaces, and \(A\) has the subspace topology as the kernel of the second. The nonarchimedean open mapping theorem [bib-KST, Lemma 3.1] therefore shows that the square defining \(A\) is strict. Our main input is that, for this finite-jet square, the strict exact row persists after completed rational localisation and is compatible with refinement. This transfers sheafiness from \(B,C,D\) to \(A\text{,}\) giving new ways for proving that these rings are sheafy. The second example shows that the failure of stable uniformity need not come from a non-reduced localisation.

    Remark 1.3. Relation with earlier work.

    🔗

    🔗

The resulting uniform sheafy domain which is not stably uniform appears to be new.

    The weighted-parity construction of Section 8 is closer to the infinite monomial-support examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara. Its additional feature is that rational localisation can be calculated in a noetherian finite-variable subring.

    Section 2 Conventions and notation

    We follow Huber for Huber pairs, adic spectra, rational subsets, and rational localisation [bib-Huber94], and use Wedhorn’s account for strong noetherianity and sheafiness [bib-Wedhorn, Proposition and Definition 6.36, Section 8.1]; the bounded-denominator language for strictness follows Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV], and the quotient presentation of a rational localisation is also discussed by Hansen–Kedlaya [bib-HK].
    🔗
    • Tate topology. Throughout, \(E\) denotes a complete Tate \(k\)-algebra. In the discretely valued setting of this paper, this means that \(E\) contains a topologically nilpotent unit and admits an open bounded \(k^\circ\)-subalgebra \(E_0\) such that
      \begin{equation*} E=E_0[1/\varpi], \qquad \{\varpi^nE_0\}_{n\geq0} \end{equation*}
      is a basis of neighbourhoods of zero. Such an \(E_0\) is called a ring of definition. We always choose it closed, hence \(\varpi\)-adically complete. A subset of \(E\) is bounded if it is contained in \(\varpi^{-r}E_0\) for some \(r\geq0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Uniformity and noetherianity. We write \(E^\circ\) for the ring of power-bounded elements. The ring \(E\) is uniform if \(E^\circ\) is bounded, and strongly noetherian if \(E\langle Z_1,\ldots,Z_s\rangle\) is noetherian for every \(s\geq0\text{.}\) A Tate ring is stably uniform if every rational localisation is uniform.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Huber pairs and sheafiness. A ring of integral elements is an open subring \(E^+\subseteq E^\circ\) which is integrally closed in \(E\text{.}\) We call the Huber pair \((E,E^+)\) sheafy when its structure presheaf, valued in complete topological rings, satisfies the sheaf condition. Thus, for a finite rational cover \(U=\bigcup_iU_i\text{,}\) restriction identifies \(\mathcal O(U)\) homeomorphically with the subspace of \(\prod_i\mathcal O(U_i)\) consisting of tuples whose restrictions to every \(U_i\cap U_j\) agree.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Strictness. We use the following bounded-denominator form of strictness. Let \(u:M\to N\) be a continuous \(k\)-linear map, and let \(M_0\subset M\) and \(N_0\subset N\) be open bounded \(k^\circ\)-submodules. Then \(u\) is strict if and only if there exists \(a\geq0\) such that
      \begin{equation*} \varpi^a\bigl(u(M)\cap N_0\bigr)\subset u(M_0). \end{equation*}
      For a surjection this becomes \(\varpi^aN_0\subset u(M_0)\text{.}\) For an injection it says that the inverse image of \(N_0\) is bounded in \(M\text{.}\) This is the criterion used by Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV, Lemma 2]. We use the term strict Milnor square in the sense of Definition 1.2; the underlying algebraic terminology is standard [bib-Milnor], [bib-KST].
      🔗
      🔗
    • Rational localisation. We take every rational datum to have at least one numerator; an empty list may be padded by \(f_1=0\text{.}\) Let \(\alpha=(f_1,\dots,f_m;g)\) be a rational datum in \(E\text{,}\) so the ideal generated by \(g,f_1,\dots,f_m\) is open. Since \(E\) is a Tate \(k\)-algebra, this ideal is the unit ideal. After multiplying the entire datum by a common power of \(\varpi\text{,}\) we may assume that all entries lie in \(E_0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      The rational localisation \(E_\alpha\) is the \(\varpi\)-adic completion of \(E[1/g]\) for the ring of definition generated by
      \begin{equation*} E_0\left[\frac{f_1}{g},\dots,\frac{f_m}{g}\right]. \end{equation*}
      For such a ring of definition \(R\text{,}\) we use the notation
      \begin{equation*} \widehat R=\varprojlim_{n\geq1}R/\varpi^nR, \qquad \ker(R\longrightarrow\widehat R)=\bigcap_{n\geq1}\varpi^nR. \end{equation*}
      \(E_\alpha\) is independent of the ring of definition and of the presentation of the rational domain, and it is transitive under rational refinement. Put
      \begin{equation*} T_E=E\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle =E_0\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle[1/\varpi], \qquad r_i=gT_i-f_i, \end{equation*}
      and
      \begin{equation*} I_{E,\alpha}:=(r_1,\dots,r_m) =\operatorname{im}\left( T_E^m\xrightarrow{\ d_{1,E}\ }T_E \right)\subset T_E, \qquad d_{1,E}(u_1,\dots,u_m)=\sum_i u_i(gT_i-f_i). \end{equation*}
      Then
      \begin{equation} E_\alpha\cong T_E/\overline{I_{E,\alpha}}.\tag{2.1} \end{equation}
      Here the bar is necessary until the ideal has been shown to be closed. This is the standard quotient presentation of rational localisation; see [bib-Huber94, Notations, (1.2), Proposition 1.3, Lemma 1.5] and [bib-HK, Definition 3.6].

    Section 3 The finite-jet ring

    In this section we identify the finite-jet algebra with a concrete subring of \(C\) and prove that it is a complete uniform domain. Recall that
    \begin{equation*} L_0=k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle, \qquad B_0=k^\circ\langle W,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} C_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle, \qquad D_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    where \(k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) denotes \(k^\circ\langle W,V\rangle/(WV-1)\text{.}\) The natural maps \(B_0\to D_0\) and \(C_0\onto D_0\) allow us to define
    \begin{equation*} A_0=B_0\times_{D_0}C_0. \end{equation*}
    We write
    \begin{equation*} A=A_0[1/\varpi],\quad L=L_0[1/\varpi],\quad B=B_0[1/\varpi],\quad C=C_0[1/\varpi],\quad D=D_0[1/\varpi]. \end{equation*}
    Each of these Tate rings carries the topology defined by the corresponding subscript-zero ring.
    🔗
    The results in this section and the next do not require the valuation on \(k\) to be discrete. They hold for any complete ultrametric normed field \(k\) and any chosen \(\varpi\in k^\times\) with \(0<|\varpi|<1\text{,}\) with the same definitions using the norm unit ball \(k^\circ\text{.}\) We return to the discretely valued hypothesis in Section 5.
    🔗
    The quotient map \(C_0\to D_0\) has a continuous \(k^\circ\)-linear section
    \begin{equation*} \sigma:D_0\longrightarrow C_0,\qquad \sigma(f_0+Qf_1)=f_0+Qf_1, \end{equation*}
    obtained by viewing the unique representative of \(Q\)-degree at most one as an element of \(C_0\text{.}\) Consequently
    \begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow A_0\longrightarrow B_0\oplus C_0 \longrightarrow D_0\longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}
    is exact, and \(A_0\) is \(\varpi\)-adically complete. After inverting \(\varpi\) we obtain a strict Milnor square
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} A&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D. \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The map \(B_0\to D_0\) is injective. Projection to \(C_0\) therefore identifies \(A_0\) with the subring
    \begin{equation} A_0= \left\{ f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h(W,W^{-1},Q): f_0,f_1\in k^\circ\langle W\rangle, \ h\in C_0 \right\}.\tag{3.1} \end{equation}
    Equivalently, \(A_0\) consists of the restricted series
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{(a,b)\in S}c_{a,b}W^aQ^b, \qquad S=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z\times\mathbb N: b\leq 1\Rightarrow a\geq 0\}, \end{equation*}
    with \(c_{a,b}\in k^\circ\text{.}\) Here restricted means that, for every \(n\text{,}\) only finitely many coefficients are nonzero modulo \(\varpi^n\text{.}\) The set \(S\) is closed under addition, so this is a subring of \(C_0\text{.}\) The same description with coefficients in \(k\) gives \(A\subset C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Equip \(A,B,C,D\) with the coefficientwise Gauss norms whose unit balls are \(A_0,B_0,C_0,D_0\text{,}\) respectively. Write
    \begin{equation*} \iota_B:A\to B,\qquad \iota_C:A\to C,\qquad \rho_B:B\to D,\qquad \rho_C:C\to D \end{equation*}
    for the structural maps, and give \(A\) the restricted Gauss norm from \(C\text{.}\) For \(a=f_0+Qf_1+Q^2h\text{,}\) the coefficient formulas give
    \begin{equation} \lVert\iota_C(a)\rVert_C=\lVert a\rVert_A, \qquad \lVert\iota_B(a)\rVert_B\leq \lVert a\rVert_A, \qquad \lVert\rho_B(b)\rVert_D\leq\lVert b\rVert_B, \qquad \lVert\rho_C(c)\rVert_D\leq\lVert c\rVert_C.\tag{3.2} \end{equation}
    Thus every structural map is norm-nonincreasing.
    🔗

    Proposition 3.1.

    The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform non-noetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain. Moreover,
    \begin{equation*} A^\circ=A_0. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    The Gauss norm on \(L=k\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) is multiplicative, and the restricted Gauss norm on \(C=L\langle Q\rangle\) is therefore multiplicative as well. In particular, \(C\) is a domain, and hence so is its subring \(A\text{.}\) The constant- and linear-coefficient maps \(C\to L\) are continuous, and \(k\langle W\rangle\) is closed in \(L=k\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) for the Gauss norm. Hence the condition that these two coefficients lie in \(k\langle W\rangle\) defines a closed subring of \(C\text{,}\) so \(A\) is complete. The image of \(\varpi\) in \(A\) is a topologically nilpotent unit, and therefore \(A\) is a Tate ring.
    🔗
    The coefficient description of \(A_0\) shows that it is exactly the unit ball for the restricted Gauss norm on \(A\text{.}\) Since this norm is multiplicative,
    \begin{equation*} \lVert a^n\rVert=\lVert a\rVert^n. \end{equation*}
    It follows that \(a\) is power-bounded if and only if \(\lVert a\rVert\leq1\text{.}\) Thus \(A^\circ=A_0\text{,}\) and the unit ball is bounded, so \(A\) is uniform.
    🔗
    To see that \(A\) is not noetherian, let \(J=\ker(\iota_B)=Q^2C\subset A\text{.}\) If \(J\) were generated by \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\text{,}\) write \(b_i\in L\) for the coefficient of \(Q^2\) in \(a_i\text{.}\) Now, for every \(\ell\in L\text{,}\) we can write
    \begin{equation*} Q^2\ell=\sum_i x_i a_i,\qquad x_i\in A, \end{equation*}
    and write \(c_i\) for the constant coefficient of \(x_i\) as a series in \(Q\text{.}\) By (3.1), we have \(c_i\in k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Since \(a_i\in Q^2C\text{,}\) its coefficients in degrees \(0\) and \(1\) vanish. The coefficient of \(Q^2\) in \(x_i a_i\) is therefore exactly \(c_i b_i\text{.}\) Comparing the coefficients of \(Q^2\) on both sides gives
    \begin{equation*} \ell=\sum_i c_i b_i,\qquad c_i\in k\langle W\rangle. \end{equation*}
    Thus \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) would generate \(L\) as a \(k\langle W\rangle\)-module. This is impossible. Indeed, it would make \(W^{-1}\) integral over \(k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Multiplying a monic relation for \(W^{-1}\) of degree \(n\) by \(W^n\) would put \(1\) in the proper ideal \((W)\subset k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Thus \(J\) is not finitely generated, and \(A\) is not noetherian.

    Section 4 A rational localisation which is not uniform

    We next compute the rational chart which witnesses the failure of stable uniformity.
    🔗
    Consider the datum
    \begin{equation*} \alpha=(W;\varpi). \end{equation*}
    The ideal \((\varpi,W)\) is open, since \(\varpi\) is a unit in \(A\text{.}\) Let
    \begin{equation*} A_\alpha=A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \end{equation*}
    be the corresponding rational localisation on which \(|W|\leq |\varpi|\text{,}\) and write \(X=W/\varpi\text{.}\) Since \(\varpi\) is already invertible in \(A\text{,}\) the algebraic localisation does not change the ring. More explicitly, \(A_\alpha\) is obtained by taking the \(\varpi\)-adic completion of \(A_0[X]\subset A\) and then inverting \(\varpi\text{.}\)
    🔗

    There is a canonical isomorphism of complete topological rings
    \begin{equation*} A_\alpha\cong k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X,\quad Q\longmapsto Q. \end{equation*}

    \begin{equation*} W^{-n}\varpi^r y\in A_0. \end{equation*}
    Multiplication by \(W^{-n}\) only introduces negative powers of \(W\) in terms of \(Q\)-degree at least two. In \(A_0[X]\text{,}\) where \(W=\varpi X\text{,}\) we therefore have
    \begin{equation*} \varpi^r y =W^n(W^{-n}\varpi^r y) =\varpi^nX^n(W^{-n}\varpi^r y)\in\varpi^nA_0[X] \end{equation*}
    for every \(n\text{.}\) Hence \(\varpi^r y\) maps to zero in \(\widehat{A_0[X]}\text{.}\) Since \(\varpi\) is invertible in \(A_\alpha\text{,}\) this proves (4.1).
    🔗
    We now construct the isomorphism. Recall that the map \(\iota_B:A\to B\) from the defining pullback is given by
    \begin{equation*} f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h\longmapsto f_0(W)+Qf_1(W); \end{equation*}
    it simply discards the terms divisible by \(Q^2\text{.}\) The substitution \(W\mapsto\varpi X\text{,}\) \(Q\mapsto Q\) defines a continuous homomorphism \(B\to T\text{.}\) Their composite is
    \begin{equation*} \theta:A\longrightarrow T,\qquad f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h\longmapsto f_0(\varpi X)+Qf_1(\varpi X). \end{equation*}
    It sends \(A_0\) into \(T_0\) and sends \(W/\varpi\) to \(X\text{.}\) It therefore sends \(A_0[X]\) into \(T_0\) and extends, first to the completions and then after inverting \(\varpi\text{,}\) to a continuous homomorphism
    \begin{equation*} \Phi:A_\alpha\longrightarrow T. \end{equation*}

    Set
    \begin{equation*} \overline X=\rho(W)/\varpi,\qquad \overline Q=\rho(Q). \end{equation*}
    Both elements are power-bounded: they belong to the completed ring of definition coming from \(A_0[X]\text{.}\) Moreover, \(\overline Q^{\,2}=0\) by (4.1). Evaluation at \(\overline X\) therefore gives a continuous homomorphism
    \begin{equation*} \Psi:T\longrightarrow A_\alpha,\qquad f+Qg\longmapsto f(\overline X)+g(\overline X)\overline Q. \end{equation*}

    It remains to check the two composites. Write \(a=f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+y\in A\text{,}\) with \(y\in Q^2C\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} (\Psi \circ \Phi)(\rho(a)) =f_0(\varpi\overline X)+\overline Qf_1(\varpi\overline X) =\rho\bigl(f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)\bigr) =\rho(a), \end{equation*}
    where the last equality uses (4.1). The image of \(A\) is dense in \(A_\alpha\text{,}\) so continuity gives \(\Psi \circ \Phi=1\text{.}\) Conversely, \(\Phi \circ \Psi\) fixes \(k\text{,}\) \(X\) and \(Q\text{,}\) and hence is the identity on the dense subring \(k[X,Q]/(Q^2)\) of \(T\text{.}\) It follows by continuity that \(\Phi\circ \Psi=1\text{.}\) Thus \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are inverse continuous homomorphisms.

For data in \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\text{,}\) membership in the corresponding rational subset is unchanged by this pullback because \(\rho_N\) is a retraction. Thus \(v\circ\rho_N\) belongs to \(U\text{,}\) and hence to some \(U_i\text{,}\) which gives \(v\in V_i\text{.}\) We may therefore apply sheafiness of the strongly noetherian ring \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) to the cover \(V=\bigcup_iV_i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Let \((x_i)_i\) be a matching family and write \(x_i=\sum_\mu x_{i,\mu}e_\mu\text{.}\) Since \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) is sheafy, the matching family \((x_{i,\mu})_i\) glues to a unique \(q_\mu\in P\) for every \(\mu\text{.}\) It remains to check that \(\lVert q_\mu\rVert\to0\text{.}\) The restrictions of the \(q_\mu\) to every \(P_i\) tend to zero because the coefficients of each \(x_i\) do. The product restriction map
    \begin{equation*} P\longrightarrow\prod_iP_i \end{equation*}
    is a topological embedding, so it reflects convergence to zero. Hence \(q_\mu\to0\text{,}\) and \(\sum_\mu q_\mu e_\mu\) is the required section over \(U\text{.}\) The same coefficientwise argument proves separation.
    🔗
    The image of the product restriction map for \(\mathcal A\) is therefore the subspace of tuples whose restrictions to every pairwise intersection agree. This subspace is closed: it is the common zero locus of the continuous difference maps, and hence is a Banach \(k\)-space. The restriction map is a continuous bijection from the Banach \(k\)-space \(\mathcal O(U)\) to this closed subspace. The nonarchimedean open mapping theorem therefore makes it a homeomorphism. We have proved the topological sheaf condition on finite rational covers. The general rational-basis theorem upgrades this to the all-open structure presheaf, and its plus-ring independence gives the same conclusion for every ring of integral elements. In particular, \((\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\) is sheafy.
    🔗
    For \(s\geq0\text{,}\) the Tate extension \(\mathcal A\langle V_1,\ldots,V_s\rangle\) is bicontinuously isomorphic to the same weighted-parity construction with the first \(s\) weights equal to zero and the remaining weights shifted by \(s\text{.}\) The argument above applies to every weight function and is independent of the ring of integral elements, so this Tate extension is sheafy for every such choice. Hence \(\mathcal A\) is strongly sheafy.
    🔗

    Remark 8.11. Admissible blow-ups.

    There is a common formal interpretation of the two bad rational charts. Let \(R_0\) denote \(A_0\) in the finite-jet example and \(\mathcal A_0\) in the weighted-parity example. The affine chart of the admissible blow-up of \(\operatorname{Spf}(R_0)\) along \((\varpi,W)\) on which the pullback of this ideal is generated by \(\varpi\) has ring
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{R_0[W/\varpi]}. \end{equation*}
    Here \(R_0[W/\varpi]\) means the subring of \(R_0[1/\varpi]\) generated by \(R_0\) and \(W/\varpi\text{,}\) and the hat denotes the \(\varpi\)-adic completion defined above. After inverting \(\varpi\text{,}\) its coordinate ring is the completed rational localisation \(R_0[1/\varpi]\langle W/\varpi\rangle\text{,}\) corresponding to \(|W|\leq|\varpi|\text{.}\) This description applies to our non-noetherian formal models because they are \(\varpi\)-adic and their ideal of definition \((\varpi)\) is principal; see [bib-FK, Chapter 0, Section 7.1(c), and Chapter II, Section 1.1(b)].
    🔗
    For \(R_0=A_0\text{,}\) the ring \(A_0[W/\varpi]\) is a domain, but the calculation in the proof of Proposition 4.1 shows that
    \begin{equation*} Q^2\in\bigcap_{n\geq1}\varpi^nA_0[W/\varpi]. \end{equation*}
    This intersection is the kernel of the completion map. Thus \(Q^2\) is killed, while \(Q\) survives, so this rational chart is non-reduced. The map from \(A\) to this completed rational localisation is also not flat by Proposition 7.1. For \(R_0=\mathcal A_0\text{,}\) the completed chart remains a domain, but its generic fibre is not uniform because the power-bounded elements \(T_n=X^{w(n)}U_n\) form an unbounded family. Thus the two examples give different failures on the same kind of affine blow-up chart.

The infinite monomial support and the unbounded power-bounded elements are in the tradition of the examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara; the approximation by the rings \(\mathcal A^{(N)}\) is what makes sheafiness accessible here.

    Section 9 How the examples were found

    The authors had for some time been interested in finding a counterexample to Problem 7 of the Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. Our attempts had centred on the example of [bib-BV, Section 4.5, Proposition 17], which is uniform but not stably uniform. We believe that this example is in fact sheafy, but we have not found a proof.
    🔗
    In January 2026 we began to experiment with AI tools, initially ChatGPT 5.2 Pro. Buzzard–Verberkmoes reduce exactness for a two-member Laurent cover to a boundedness condition on the relevant rings of definition [bib-BV, Lemma 2]. For a uniform ring this follows from boundedness of the power-bounded elements [bib-BV, Lemma 3 and Corollary 4]. Their reduction from general rational covers to Laurent covers is given in [bib-BV, Lemma 8 and the proof of Theorem 7]. For sheafiness these checks must also be made after rational localisation. Their Theorem 7 cannot be applied directly to Proposition 17, since one of the rational localisations in that example is not uniform. Our plan was instead to ask ChatGPT to write down many explicit rational covers and check the required exactness directly for each one, in the hope that a pattern, and eventually a proof, would emerge.
    🔗
    After a couple of weeks ChatGPT 5.2 Pro claimed to have a complete proof. The authors then began the slow process of understanding this proof, only to discover, with the help of ChatGPT 5.3, that one of its arguments used the following assertion:
    🔗
    If \(\phi:A\to B\) is a continuous homomorphism of Hausdorff topological abelian groups and \(A\) is complete, then \(\phi(A)\) is closed in \(B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    This assertion is false, and its use completely broke the purported proof.
    🔗
    Motivated by this, we began in parallel to use Claude Code to formalise the parts of the theory of adic spaces needed here, including the theorem that strongly noetherian Tate rings are sheafy. The aim was a practical one: future AI-generated proofs could be formalised before the human authors had spent days understanding an argument containing a fatal error near its end.
    🔗
    We then returned to writing down covers and looking for a pattern. This continued through ChatGPT 5.3 and subsequent versions. When ChatGPT 5.6 Sol became available, we asked, almost in passing, whether it could prove the result we wanted or find a new example. It produced the examples studied here. By then the Lean development was far enough advanced that we could use Claude Code to formalise the arguments quickly in Lean and check that they were correct.
    🔗
    At the time of writing, it is still possible to reproduce this by asking the agents to find an example of a uniform and sheafy non-noetherian ring which is not stably uniform. One point to note is that they sometimes reply that this is an open problem, which prevents them from proceeding. In such cases, saying an example exists; find it often produces an example.

    Subsection A.7 Mathematical and code provenance

    The definitions of rational localisation, the structure presheaf and the sheaf condition follow Huber [bib-Huber94] and Wedhorn [bib-Wedhorn]. The independence of the choice of ring of integral elements uses the standard rational-refinement formalism described by Kedlaya [bib-KedlayaAWS, Lemma 1.6.8 and Remark 1.6.9].
    🔗
    The restricted-power-series implementation includes code adapted from William Coram’s development [bib-CoramCode]. Its univariate Gauss-norm layer uses Fabrizio Barroero’s mathlib code [bib-BarroeroCode], while the multivariable power-series equivalences use code of Bingyu Xia [bib-XiaCode]. These are separate sources, and their attribution is retained in the corresponding source-file headers.
