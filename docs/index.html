    Uniform sheafy Tate domains that are not stably uniform

    Anonymous
    Abstract
    Work of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and, independently, Mihara shows that stable uniformity is a sufficient criterion for a Tate Huber ring to be sheafy, meaning that the structure presheaf of topological rings on its associated adic space is a sheaf. In this paper we construct examples of uniform sheafy rings which are not stably uniform, answering Question 7 of Kedlaya’s Nonarchimedean Scottish Book. The examples were found in collaboration with ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. The first has been fully formalised in Lean 4; its proof rests on showing that a particular strict pullback of complete Tate rings is preserved by rational localisation. The second, whose bad localisation remains an integral domain, is proved here but has not yet been formalised.
    🔗
    🔗

    Section 1 Introduction

    Buzzard and Verberkmoes proved that every stably uniform Tate Huber ring is sheafy and exhibited both uniform rings which are not stably uniform and uniform rings which are not sheafy [bib-BV]. Hansen and Kedlaya subsequently asked whether a uniform sheafy Huber ring must be stably uniform [bib-HK, Remark 3.16]; this is also Question 7 of Kedlaya’s Nonarchimedean Scottish Book [bib-Scottish]. We give a negative answer in two forms.
    🔗
    We begin with the finite-jet pullback. This is a nonnoetherian uniform integral domain for which a distinguished rational localisation is nonreduced, and hence is no longer uniform. Let \(k\) be a complete discretely valued nonarchimedean field, with valuation ring \(k^\circ\text{,}\) uniformizer \(\varpi\text{,}\) and residue field \(\widetilde{k}\text{.}\) Put
    \begin{equation*} L_0=k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle, \qquad B_0=k^\circ\langle W,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} C_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle, \qquad D_0=L_0\langle Q\rangle/(Q^2), \end{equation*}
    where \(k^\circ\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) means \(k^\circ\langle W,V\rangle/(WV-1)\text{.}\) There are natural maps \(B_0\to D_0\) and \(C_0\onto D_0\text{.}\) Define
    \begin{equation*} A_0=B_0\times_{D_0}C_0, \qquad A=A_0[1/\varpi], \end{equation*}
    and endow \(A\) with the topology defined by the ring of definition \(A_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The main result is the following.
    🔗

    Theorem 1.1.

    The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform nonnoetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain, with \(A^\circ=A_0\text{.}\) The Huber pair \((A,A^\circ)\) is sheafy. However,
    \begin{equation*} A\left\langle \frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \cong k\langle X,Q\rangle/(Q^2), \qquad X=\frac{W}{\varpi}, \end{equation*}
    so \(A\) is not stably uniform.

    🔗
    The proof uses Milnor squares of complete Tate rings. Recall that a cartesian square of rings with a surjective leg is called a Milnor square [bib-Milnor, Section 2]; the complete Tate setting is used by Kerz–Saito–Tamme in their work on analytic \(K\)-theory [bib-KST, Section 3].
    🔗

    Definition 1.2.

    A commutative square of complete Tate \(k\)-algebras
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} R&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    is a strict Milnor square if it is cartesian, the sequence of underlying topological \(k\)-vector spaces
    \begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow B\oplus C \xrightarrow{\ (b,c)\mapsto \bar b-\bar c\ }D\longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}
    is strict exact, and \(C\to D\) is a strict surjection.
    🔗
    🔗
    After rotating the square if necessary, the initial Milnor square is strict by the nonarchimedean open mapping theorem [bib-KST, Lemma 3.1]. Our main input is that strictness persists after completed rational localisation and is compatible with refinement. This allows sheafiness to be transferred from \(B,C,D\) to \(R\text{.}\) The associated square of affinoid adic spaces is a pushout among affinoid spaces, but not among all adic spaces; see Remark 6.3. The second example shows that the failure of stable uniformity need not come from a nonreduced localisation.
    🔗
    Both examples were found in collaboration with ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. The first has been formalised in Lean; Appendix A describes the statements used and their relation with the definitions in this paper.
    🔗

    Remark 1.3. Relation with earlier work.

    Buzzard–Verberkmoes construct a uniform affinoid ring with a nonuniform rational localisation and, separately, a uniform affinoid ring whose structure presheaf is not a sheaf [bib-BV, Propositions 17 and 18]. Mihara gives further examples of both phenomena [bib-Mihara]. These examples do not give a uniform sheafy ring which is not stably uniform.
    🔗
    The strict Koszul result used in Lemma 5.1 is due to Ben-Bassat–Kremnizer and Bambozzi–Kremnizer [bib-BBK, Definition 5.7 and Lemmas 5.13--5.14] [bib-BK, Definitions 4.5--4.6, Proposition 4.9, Lemma 4.11, and Corollary 4.13]. We use its lattice form to prove that rational localisation preserves the finite-jet Milnor square. The resulting uniform sheafy domain which is not stably uniform appears to be new.
    🔗
    The weighted-parity construction of Section 7 is closer to the infinite monomial-support examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara. Its additional feature is that rational localisation can be calculated in a noetherian finite head.

    Section 2 Conventions and notation

    We follow Huber for Huber pairs, adic spectra, rational subsets, and rational localisation [bib-Huber94], and use Wedhorn’s account for strong noetherianity and sheafiness [bib-Wedhorn, Proposition and Definition 6.36, Section 8.1]; the bounded-denominator language for strictness follows Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV], and the quotient presentation of a rational localisation is also discussed by Hansen–Kedlaya [bib-HK].
    🔗
    Throughout, \(E\) denotes a complete Tate \(k\)-algebra. In the discretely valued setting of this paper, this means that \(E\) contains a topologically nilpotent unit and admits an open bounded \(k^\circ\)-subalgebra \(E_0\) such that
    \begin{equation*} E=E_0[1/\varpi], \qquad \{\varpi^nE_0\}_{n\geq0} \end{equation*}
    is a basis of neighbourhoods of zero. Such an \(E_0\) is called a ring of definition. We always choose it closed, hence \(\varpi\)-adically complete. A subset of \(E\) is bounded if it is contained in \(\varpi^{-r}E_0\) for some \(r\geq0\text{.}\) If \(M\) is a complete topological \(k\)-vector space, an open bounded \(k^\circ\)-submodule \(M_0\subset M\) will be called a lattice.
    🔗
    We write \(E^\circ\) for the ring of power-bounded elements. The ring \(E\) is uniform if \(E^\circ\) is bounded, and strongly noetherian if \(E\langle Z_1,\ldots,Z_s\rangle\) is noetherian for every \(s\geq0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A ring of integral elements is an open subring \(E^+\subseteq E^\circ\) which is integrally closed in \(E\text{.}\) We call the Huber pair \((E,E^+)\) sheafy when its structure presheaf is a sheaf of complete topological rings; in particular, the map to the matching-family equaliser for a finite rational cover is a homeomorphism. We call \(E\) strongly sheafy if every \(E\langle Z_1,\ldots,Z_s\rangle\text{,}\) with its maximal ring of integral elements, is sheafy.
    🔗
    We use the following bounded-denominator form of strictness. Let \(u:M\to N\) be a continuous \(k\)-linear map and choose lattices \(M_0\subset M\) and \(N_0\subset N\text{.}\) Then \(u\) is strict if and only if there exists \(a\geq0\) such that
    \begin{equation} \varpi^a\bigl(u(M)\cap N_0\bigr)\subset u(M_0).\tag{2.1} \end{equation}
    For a surjection this becomes \(\varpi^aN_0\subset u(M_0)\text{.}\) For an injection it says that the lattice induced from the target is bounded in the source. This is the criterion used by Buzzard–Verberkmoes [bib-BV, Lemma 2]. We use the term strict Milnor square in the sense of Definition 1.2; the underlying algebraic terminology is standard [bib-Milnor], [bib-KST].
    🔗
    We take every rational datum to have at least one numerator; an empty list may be padded by \(f_1=0\text{.}\) Let \(\alpha=(f_1,\dots,f_m;g)\) be a rational datum in \(E\text{,}\) so the ideal generated by \(g,f_1,\dots,f_m\) is open. Since \(E\) is a Tate \(k\)-algebra, this ideal is the unit ideal. After multiplying the entire datum by a common power of \(\varpi\text{,}\) we may assume that all entries lie in \(E_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The rational localisation \(E_\alpha\) is the separated \(\varpi\)-adic completion of \(E[1/g]\) for the ring of definition generated by
    \begin{equation*} E_0\left[\frac{f_1}{g},\dots,\frac{f_m}{g}\right]. \end{equation*}
    It is independent of the ring of definition and of the presentation of the rational domain, and it is transitive under rational refinement. Put
    \begin{equation*} T_E=E\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle =E_0\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle[1/\varpi], \qquad r_i=gT_i-f_i, \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation} I_{E,\alpha}:=(r_1,\dots,r_m) =\operatorname{im}\left( T_E^m\xrightarrow{\ d_{1,E}\ }T_E \right)\subset T_E, \qquad d_{1,E}(u_1,\dots,u_m)=\sum_i u_i(gT_i-f_i),\tag{2.2} \end{equation}
    Then
    \begin{equation} E_\alpha\cong T_E/\overline{I_{E,\alpha}}.\tag{2.3} \end{equation}
    Here the bar is necessary until the ideal has been shown to be closed. This is the standard quotient presentation of rational localisation; see [bib-Huber94, (1.2), Proposition 1.3, Lemma 1.5] and [bib-HK, Definition 3.6].
    🔗
    A Tate ring is stably uniform if every rational localisation is uniform.

    Section 3 The global ring

    In this section we identify the finite-jet algebra with a concrete support subring and prove that it is a complete uniform domain.
    🔗
    Set
    \begin{equation*} L=L_0[1/\varpi],\quad B=B_0[1/\varpi],\quad C=C_0[1/\varpi],\quad D=D_0[1/\varpi]. \end{equation*}
    The map \(C_0\to D_0\) has a continuous \(k^\circ\)-linear section given by truncation in the \(Q\)-variable:
    \begin{equation*} f_0+Qf_1\longmapsto f_0+Qf_1. \end{equation*}
    Consequently
    \begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow A_0\longrightarrow B_0\oplus C_0 \longrightarrow D_0\longrightarrow 0\tag{3.1} \end{equation}
    is exact, and \(A_0\) is \(\varpi\)-adically complete. After inverting \(\varpi\) we obtain a strict Milnor square
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} A&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D. \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    In fact, the chosen rings of definition already make the integral row exact, so no powers of \(\varpi\) are lost in the defining square.
    🔗
    The map \(B_0\to D_0\) is injective. Projection to \(C_0\) therefore identifies \(A_0\) with the subring
    \begin{equation} A_0= \left\{ f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h(W,W^{-1},Q): f_0,f_1\in k^\circ\langle W\rangle, \ h\in C_0 \right\}.\tag{3.2} \end{equation}
    Equivalently, \(A_0\) consists of the restricted series
    \begin{equation} \sum_{(a,b)\in S}c_{a,b}W^aQ^b, \qquad S=\{(a,b)\in\mathbf Z\times\mathbf N: b\leq 1\Rightarrow a\geq 0\},\tag{3.3} \end{equation}
    with \(c_{a,b}\in k^\circ\text{.}\) Here restricted means that, for every \(n\text{,}\) only finitely many coefficients are nonzero modulo \(\varpi^n\text{.}\) The set \(S\) is closed under addition, so this is a subring of \(C_0\text{.}\) The same description with coefficients in \(k\) gives \(A\subset C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Equip \(A,B,C,D\) with the coefficientwise Gauss norms whose unit balls are the displayed rings of definition. Write
    \begin{equation*} \iota_B:A\to B,\qquad \iota_C:A\to C,\qquad \rho_B:B\to D,\qquad \rho_C:C\to D \end{equation*}
    for the structural maps, and give \(A\) the restricted Gauss norm from \(C\text{.}\) For \(a=f_0+Qf_1+Q^2h\text{,}\) the coefficient formulas give
    \begin{equation} \lVert\iota_C(a)\rVert_C=\lVert a\rVert_A, \qquad \lVert\iota_B(a)\rVert_B\leq \lVert a\rVert_A, \qquad \lVert\rho_B(b)\rVert_D\leq\lVert b\rVert_B, \qquad \lVert\rho_C(c)\rVert_D\leq\lVert c\rVert_C.\tag{3.4} \end{equation}
    Thus every structural map is norm-nonincreasing.
    🔗

    Proposition 3.1.

    The ring \(A\) is a complete uniform nonnoetherian Tate \(k\)-algebra and an integral domain. Moreover,
    \begin{equation*} A^\circ=A_0. \end{equation*}

    Proof.
    For \(0\neq c\in C\text{,}\) define
    \begin{equation*} \nu(c)=\max\{n\in\mathbf Z:c\in\varpi^nC_0\}. \end{equation*}
    This maximum exists because \(C_0\) is \(\varpi\)-adically separated and \(C=C_0[1/\varpi]\text{.}\) We claim that
    \begin{equation} \nu(cc')=\nu(c)+\nu(c') \qquad (c,c'\neq 0).\tag{3.5} \end{equation}
    After multiplying \(c\) and \(c'\) by suitable powers of \(\varpi\text{,}\) it is enough to treat the case \(c,c'\in C_0\setminus\varpi C_0\text{.}\) Their reductions are nonzero elements of
    \begin{equation*} C_0/\varpi C_0\cong\widetilde{k}[W,W^{-1},Q]. \end{equation*}
    This is a domain, so the product of the reductions is nonzero. This proves (3.5); in particular, \(C\) is a domain.
    🔗
    The support description gives
    \begin{equation*} A_0=A\cap C_0. \end{equation*}
    Every element of \(A_0\) is power-bounded because \(A_0\) is a subring. Conversely, let \(a\in A\setminus A_0\text{.}\) Then \(\nu(a)<0\text{,}\) and by (3.5), \(\nu(a^n)=n\nu(a)\text{.}\) Hence the powers of \(a\) cannot all lie in any fixed \(\varpi^{-r}A_0\text{.}\) Thus \(a\) is not power-bounded. It follows that \(A^\circ=A_0\text{,}\) so \(A\) is uniform. Completeness follows from the completeness of \(A_0\text{,}\) and \(A\) is a domain because it is a subring of \(C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    For nonnoetherianity, let \(J=\ker(\iota_B)=Q^2C\subset A\text{.}\) If \(J\) were generated by \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\text{,}\) then, for every \(\ell\in L\text{,}\) comparison of the \(Q^2\)-coefficient in an expression
    \begin{equation*} Q^2\ell=\sum_i x_i a_i,\qquad x_i\in A, \end{equation*}
    would express \(\ell\) as a \(k\langle W\rangle\)-linear combination of the \(Q^2\)-coefficients of the \(a_i\text{.}\) This would make \(L=k\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\) a finite \(k\langle W\rangle\)-module, which is impossible: a finite algebra is integral, whereas a monic equation for \(W^{-1}\text{,}\) after multiplication by a power of \(W\text{,}\) would put \(1\) in the proper ideal \((W)\subset k\langle W\rangle\text{.}\) Thus \(J\) is not finitely generated, and \(A\) is not noetherian.

    Section 4 A nonuniform rational localisation

    We next compute the rational chart which witnesses the failure of stable uniformity.
    🔗
    Consider the datum
    \begin{equation*} \alpha_{\mathrm{in}}=(W;\varpi). \end{equation*}
    The ideal \((\varpi,W)\) is open, since \(\varpi\) is a unit in \(A\text{.}\) Let
    \begin{equation*} A_{\mathrm{in}}=A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \end{equation*}
    be the corresponding rational localisation on \(|W|\leq |\varpi|\text{,}\) and write \(X=W/\varpi\text{.}\) By the ring-of-definition construction, \(A_{\mathrm{in}}\) is the separated completion of the subring generated by \(A_0\) and \(X\text{.}\)

    Section 5 Localisation of the Milnor square

    In this section we prove that rational localisation preserves the defining pullback. We first record the strict Koszul estimate needed for the proof.
    🔗

    Subsection 5.1 Koszul complexes for rational localisations

    Let \(E\) be an affinoid \(k\)-algebra, choose a noetherian \(\varpi\)-adically complete ring of definition \(E_0\subset E\text{,}\) and let \(f_1,\dots,f_m,g\in E_0\) generate the unit ideal in \(E\text{.}\) Put
    \begin{equation*} T_E=E\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle, \qquad T_{E,0}=E_0\langle T_1,\dots,T_m\rangle, \end{equation*}
    and set \(r_i=gT_i-f_i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    We give \(T_E\) the topology for which \(\{\varpi^nT_{E,0}\}_{n\geq0}\) is a basis of neighbourhoods of zero. The term \(\bigwedge^qT_E^m\) has the finite-module topology defined by the lattice \(\bigwedge^qT_{E,0}^m\text{;}\) equivalently, in the standard exterior basis it has the finite product topology. Images have the subspace topology. Thus a differential is strict when it is open onto its image, or equivalently when its coimage is homeomorphic to its image.
    🔗
    The following strict Koszul result is known [bib-BBK, Definition 5.7 and Lemmas 5.13--5.14] [bib-BK, Definitions 4.5--4.6, Proposition 4.9, Lemma 4.11, and Corollary 4.13]. We state the two bounded-denominator consequences which will be used in the pullback argument.
    🔗

    Lemma 5.1.

    The Koszul complex
    \begin{equation*} 0\longrightarrow \bigwedge^mT_E^m\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow\bigwedge^2T_E^m\xrightarrow{d_{2,E}} T_E^m\xrightarrow{d_{1,E}}T_E \end{equation*}
    is exact in positive degrees. Every differential is continuous and strict onto its image, and every image is closed. In particular, if
    \begin{equation*} I_E=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)=\im(d_{1,E})\subset T_E, \qquad d_{1,E}(u_1,\ldots,u_m)=\sum_i u_ir_i, \end{equation*}
    then \(I_E\) is closed. Moreover, there exists \(h_E\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}\) such that
    \begin{equation} \varpi^{h_E}(I_E\cap T_{E,0}) \subset d_{1,E}(T_{E,0}^m).\tag{5.1} \end{equation}
    There also exists \(z_E\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}\) such that
    \begin{equation} \varpi^{z_E}\bigl(\ker(d_{1,E})\cap T_{E,0}^m\bigr) \subset d_{2,E}\bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge^2 T_{E,0}^m\bigr).\tag{5.2} \end{equation}

    Subsection 5.2 The defining ideals under pullback

    Fix a rational datum
    \begin{equation*} \alpha=(f_1,\dots,f_m;g) \end{equation*}
    in \(A\text{.}\) Multiplying all entries by a common power of \(\varpi\text{,}\) we assume \(f_1,\dots,f_m,g\in A_0\text{.}\) For \(E\in\{A,B,C,D\}\) use the notation \(T_E\text{,}\) \(T_{E,0}\text{,}\) and \(d_{1,E}\) introduced above, and write
    \begin{equation*} I_E=(gT_1-f_1,\ldots,gT_m-f_m)\subset T_E. \end{equation*}

    Lemma 6.1. Sheaf transfer.

    Let
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} R&\longrightarrow&C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ B&\longrightarrow&D \end{matrix} \end{equation*}

    \begin{equation} 0\longrightarrow \cO_R(U)\longrightarrow \cO_B(U_B)\oplus\cO_C(U_C) \longrightarrow\cO_D(U_D)\longrightarrow 0.\tag{6.1} \end{equation}

    🔗

    Proof.
    We first work on the rational basis. Let \(U=\bigcup_{i=1}^nU_i\) be a finite rational covering. For \(E\in\{R,B,C,D\}\text{,}\) let
    \begin{equation*} r_E:\cO_E(U_E)\longrightarrow\prod_i\cO_E((U_i)_E) \end{equation*}
    denote product restriction, using \(U_R=U\) and \((U_i)_R=U_i\) as specified in the statement. The first map in (6.1) is a topological embedding, and the same is true after replacing \(U\) by any \(U_i\text{.}\) Since \(B\) and \(C\) are sheafy, \(r_B\) and \(r_C\) are topological embeddings. Naturality gives a commutative square
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} \cO_R(U)&\longrightarrow&\cO_B(U_B)\oplus\cO_C(U_C)\\ \downarrow r_R&&\downarrow r_B\oplus r_C\\ \prod_i\cO_R(U_i)&\longrightarrow& \prod_i\bigl(\cO_B((U_i)_B)\oplus\cO_C((U_i)_C)\bigr). \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    The top and right arrows are topological embeddings, as is the bottom arrow. It follows that \(r_R\) is a topological embedding.
    🔗
    Now let \(x_i\in\cO_R(U_i)\) be a matching family. Its images in the \(B\)- and \(C\)-section rings glue to elements
    \begin{equation*} b\in\cO_B(U_B), \qquad c\in\cO_C(U_C). \end{equation*}
    Their images in \(\cO_D(U_D)\) agree after restriction to every \((U_i)_D\text{.}\) Since the structure presheaf for \(D\) is separated, they agree globally. Exactness on \(U\) therefore gives \(x\in\cO_R(U)\) mapping to \((b,c)\text{.}\) Exactness on each \(U_i\) shows that \(x|_{U_i}=x_i\text{,}\) and uniqueness follows in the same way. To retain the topology on arbitrary opens, let \(V=\bigcup_\lambda V_\lambda\) be an open covering and let \(M\) be its matching-family equalizer with the subspace topology. For every rational \(U\subset V\text{,}\) quasi-compactness gives a finite rational cover of \(U\) subordinate to the \(V_\lambda\text{.}\) Restriction from \(M\) to that finite cover, followed by the rational-basis result, gives a continuous map \(M\to\cO_R(U)\text{,}\) independent of the chosen subordinate cover. These maps are compatible under rational restriction. Since
    \begin{equation*} \cO_R(V)=\varprojlim_{U\subset V,\ U\ \mathrm{rational}}\cO_R(U) \end{equation*}
    with its projective-limit topology, the inverse of the algebraic gluing map \(\cO_R(V)\to M\) is continuous. Hence the gluing map is a homeomorphism, as required.

    Remark 6.3. Affinoid versus global pushouts.

    The coefficient descriptions of the maximal plus rings give
    \begin{equation*} A^\circ=B^\circ\times_{D^\circ}C^\circ. \end{equation*}
    Indeed, a compatible pair which is power-bounded in \(B\) and \(C\) has its constant and linear \(Q\)-coefficients in both \(k\langle W\rangle\) and \(L_0\text{;}\) their intersection is \(k^\circ\langle W\rangle\text{,}\) while the higher \(Q\)-tail is already integral in \(C_0\text{.}\) Together with the pullback topology, this says that the defining square is a pullback of complete Huber pairs.
    🔗
    Put \(X_E=\Spa(E,E^\circ)\) for \(E\in\{A,B,C,D\}\text{.}\) Huber’s affinoid mapping theorem identifies morphisms between affinoid adic spaces with continuous morphisms of the corresponding complete Huber pairs [bib-Huber94, Proposition 2.1].     \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Hom}(X_A,Z)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(X_B,Z) \times_{\operatorname{Hom}(X_D,Z)} \operatorname{Hom}(X_C,Z) \end{equation*}
    is bijective. Thus the contravariant square
    \begin{equation*} \begin{matrix} X_D&\longrightarrow&X_C\\ \downarrow&&\downarrow\\ X_B&\longrightarrow&X_A \end{matrix} \end{equation*}
    is a pushout in the full subcategory of affinoid adic spaces.
    🔗
    This does not make it a pushout in the category of all adic spaces: there the same map must be bijective for an arbitrary, possibly nonaffinoid, target \(Z\text{.}\) In fact, uniqueness already fails for an explicit nonaffinoid target. Let
    \begin{equation*} U_1=\{x\in X_A:|Q^2(x)|\leq |\varpi(x)|\},\qquad U_2=\{x\in X_A:|1|\leq |W(x)|\},\qquad U=U_1\cup U_2. \end{equation*}
    The map \(X_B\to X_A\) factors through \(U_1\text{,}\) because \(Q^2=0\) in \(B\text{,}\) while \(X_C\to X_A\) factors through \(U_2\text{,}\) because both \(W\) and \(W^{-1}\) are power-bounded in \(C\) and hence \(|W|=1\) on \(X_C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The open subset \(U\) is proper. Use additive valuations with values in \(\Gamma=\mathbf Z^3_{\mathrm{lex}}\) and, for a nonzero restricted series in the support description (3.3), put
    \begin{equation*} v\left(\sum c_{a,b}W^aQ^b\right) =\min_{\mathrm{lex}}\{(v_k(c_{a,b}),b,a):c_{a,b}\neq0\}. \end{equation*}
    Restrictedness makes the minimum exist, and the initial-term argument for the lexicographic monomial order makes \(v\) multiplicative. The support condition makes it nonnegative on \(A_0=A^\circ\text{,}\) while \(v(\varpi)>0\text{,}\) so it is continuous and defines a point \(x\in X_A\text{.}\) Since
    \begin{equation*} v(Q^2)=(0,2,0)<(1,0,0)=v(\varpi),\qquad v(W)=(0,0,1)>0, \end{equation*}
    one has \(|Q^2(x)|>|\varpi(x)|\) and \(|W(x)|<1\text{;}\) hence \(x\notin U\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Adic spaces may be glued along open subspaces. Glue two copies of \(X_A\) along \(U\) and write \(Z=X_A^{(1)}\cup_U X_A^{(2)}\text{.}\) The two open immersions \(j_1,j_2:X_A\to Z\) agree on \(U\text{,}\) so they agree after precomposition with both \(X_B\to X_A\) and \(X_C\to X_A\text{,}\) but they are distinct at the two unglued copies of \(x\text{.}\) The displayed Hom map is therefore not injective for this \(Z\text{.}\) Thus \(X_A\) is not the pushout in the full category of adic spaces. More generally, the same doubling argument shows that a cocone whose two legs factor through a proper open subspace of its vertex cannot be a pushout in that category. We do not know whether the original span admits some other, necessarily nonaffinoid, pushout object.

    There is a canonical isometric isomorphism
    \begin{equation} \mathcal A\left\langle\frac{W}{\varpi}\right\rangle \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal B, \qquad W\longmapsto\varpi X.\tag{7.14} \end{equation}
    The ideal \((W-\varpi X)\) in \(\mathcal A\langle X\rangle\) is closed, so no additional separated quotient is required.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    On rings of definition define
    \begin{equation*} q_0:\mathcal A_0\langle X\rangle\longrightarrow\mathcal B_0, \qquad q_0(W^aX^cU^\nu)=\varpi^aX^{a+c}U^\nu. \end{equation*}
    For a target monomial define a \(k^\circ\)-linear section by
    \begin{equation} s\bigl(b_{d,\nu}X^dU^\nu\bigr) =\varpi^{-\omega(\nu)}b_{d,\nu} W^{\omega(\nu)}X^{d-\omega(\nu)}U^\nu.\tag{7.15} \end{equation}
    The divisibility and support conditions in (7.12) make this well-defined, and (7.13) shows that \(s\) is isometric. Moreover, \(q_0s=\operatorname{id}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    It remains to identify the kernel. Let \(M=W^aX^cU^\nu\) and put \(r=a-\omega(\nu)\geq0\text{.}\) If \(r>0\text{,}\) set
    \begin{equation*} D(M)=W^{\omega(\nu)}X^cU^\nu \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}W^{r-1-j}(\varpi X)^j, \end{equation*}
    and set \(D(M)=0\) when \(r=0\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation} M-sq_0(M)=(W-\varpi X)D(M).\tag{7.16} \end{equation}
    Each basis monomial occurring in \(D(M)\) is still allowed in \(\mathcal A_0\langle X\rangle\text{,}\) and all its scalar coefficients have norm at most one. Thus \(D\) is norm-nonincreasing on finite-support series and extends continuously to the \(c_0\)-completion. If \(x\in\ker(q_0)\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} x=x-sq_0(x)=(W-\varpi X)D(x). \end{equation*}
    The reverse inclusion is immediate, so
    \begin{equation} \ker(q_0)=(W-\varpi X)\mathcal A_0\langle X\rangle.\tag{7.17} \end{equation}
    Since \(q_0\) is a split surjection onto the complete ring \(\mathcal B_0\text{,}\) this kernel is closed. The quotient norm is exactly (7.13): boundedness of \(q_0\) gives one inequality, and the isometric section gives the reverse inequality. Inverting \(\varpi\) proves (7.14) and the closedness assertion.

    Proposition 7.6.

    The ring \(\mathcal B\) is an integral domain but is not uniform.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    The weighted convergence condition in (7.12) implies ordinary restricted convergence, because \(|b_{d,\nu}|\leq|\varpi|^{\omega(\nu)} |\varpi|^{-\omega(\nu)}|b_{d,\nu}|\text{.}\) Hence coefficientwise inclusion gives an injective homomorphism
    \begin{equation*} \mathcal B\hookrightarrow k\langle X,U_1,U_2,\ldots\rangle. \end{equation*}
    The target is a domain by the Gauss-norm argument used in Proposition 7.3; therefore \(\mathcal B\) is a domain.
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} T_n=\varpi^{-n}Y_n=X^nU_n\in\mathcal B. \end{equation*}

    \begin{equation} \lVert T_n^{2r}\rVert_{\mathrm{in}}=1, \qquad \lVert T_n^{2r+1}\rVert_{\mathrm{in}}=|\varpi|^{-n}.\tag{7.18} \end{equation}

    Subsection 7.3 Small perturbations of rational data

    The finite-head argument rests on the following elementary stability result.
    🔗

    Lemma 7.7. Small perturbation lemma.

    Let \((E,E^+)\) be a complete Tate Huber pair and choose a closed ring of definition \(E_0\subseteq E^+\text{.}\) Let \(\alpha=(f_1,\ldots,f_m;g)\) be a rational datum whose entries lie in \(E_0\text{.}\) Write \(d_0=g\) and \(d_i=f_i\) for \(i\geq1\text{.}\) Suppose that for some \(\ell\geq0\) there are \(a_0,\ldots,a_m\in E_0\) with
    \begin{equation} \varpi^\ell=\sum_{j=0}^m a_jd_j.\tag{7.19} \end{equation}
    Let \(d_j'\in E_0\) satisfy
    \begin{equation} d_j'-d_j\in\varpi^{\ell+1}E_0 \qquad(0\leq j\leq m),\tag{7.20} \end{equation}
    and write \(\alpha'=(f_1',\ldots,f_m';g')\text{.}\) Then \(\alpha'\) is a rational datum, \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha'\) define the same rational subset of \(\Spa(E,E^+)\text{,}\) and their completed rational localisations are canonically isomorphic.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Write \(d_j'-d_j=\varpi^{\ell+1}h_j\) with \(h_j\in E_0\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \sum_j a_jd_j' =\varpi^\ell(1+\varpi H), \qquad H=\sum_j a_jh_j\in E_0. \end{equation*}
    The element \(1+\varpi H\) is a unit of \(E_0\text{,}\) with inverse given by its convergent geometric series. Multiplying the \(a_j\) by this inverse gives an integral Bezout relation for the primed datum; in particular, \(\alpha'\) is open.
    🔗
    Let \(x\) lie in the rational subset defined by \(\alpha\text{.}\) Since \(a_j\in E_0\subseteq E^+\text{,}\) relation (7.19) gives
    \begin{equation*} |\varpi|^\ell\leq |g(x)|. \end{equation*}
    The perturbations have value at most \(|\varpi|^{\ell+1}<|g(x)|\text{,}\) so \(|g'(x)|=|g(x)|\) and \(|f_i'(x)|\leq|g'(x)|\text{.}\) This proves one inclusion of rational subsets. Applying the same argument to the primed Bezout relation proves the reverse inclusion.
    🔗
    Let \(E_\alpha\) be the first rational localisation and put
    \begin{equation*} q=\frac{\varpi^\ell}{g} =a_0+\sum_i a_i\frac{f_i}{g}\in E_\alpha^\circ. \end{equation*}
    Then
    \begin{equation*} \frac{g'}g=1+\varpi h_0q. \end{equation*}
    The second summand is topologically nilpotent. After adjoining the finitely many power-bounded elements involved to a ring of definition, the geometric series for its inverse and all powers of that inverse lie in the same bounded subring. Thus \(g'/g\) is a \(1\)-unit with power-bounded inverse, and
    \begin{equation*} \frac{f_i'}{g'} =\frac{f_i/g+\varpi h_iq}{1+\varpi h_0q} \end{equation*}
    is power-bounded. The universal property gives a homomorphism \(E_{\alpha'}\to E_\alpha\text{.}\) Repeating the argument with the primed Bezout relation gives a homomorphism in the opposite direction. Both composites restrict to the identity on \(E\text{;}\) universal-property uniqueness makes them inverse. The isomorphism is therefore canonical.

    Subsection 7.4 Finite-head rational localisation

    Fix \(N\geq1\text{.}\) For a tail multi-index \(\mu=(\mu_n)_{n>N}\in\mathbf N^{(\{n>N\})}\text{,}\) write \(\mu_n=2q_n+\epsilon_n\) with \(\epsilon_n\in\{0,1\}\) and put
    \begin{equation} e_\mu=\prod_{n>N}Y_n^{\epsilon_n}Z_n^{q_n}.\tag{7.21} \end{equation}
    The finite-stage normal forms give an isometric Banach-module decomposition
    \begin{equation} \mathcal A\cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{\,c_0}\mathcal A_Ne_\mu.\tag{7.22} \end{equation}
    Multiplication is determined by
    \begin{equation} e_\mu e_\lambda =W^{\omega(\mu)+\omega(\lambda)-\omega(\mu+\lambda)}e_{\mu+\lambda}.\tag{7.23} \end{equation}
    Here \(\omega\) is applied only to the tail coordinates. Projection to the coefficient of \(e_0\) is a norm-nonincreasing algebra retraction
    \begin{equation} \rho_N:\mathcal A\longrightarrow\mathcal A_N.\tag{7.24} \end{equation}
    🔗

    Proposition 7.8. Coefficientwise localisation.

    Let \(\alpha\) be a rational datum in \(\mathcal A_N\text{,}\) and put \(P=(\mathcal A_N)_\alpha\text{.}\) There is a canonical topological algebra isomorphism
    \begin{equation} \mathcal A_\alpha \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{\,c_0}Pe_\mu.\tag{7.25} \end{equation}
    It is natural for rational refinements represented in a finite head: if \(P\to Q\) is the corresponding head restriction map, the induced restriction sends
    \begin{equation} \sum_\mu p_\mu e_\mu \longmapsto \sum_\mu (p_\mu|_Q)e_\mu.\tag{7.26} \end{equation}
    The same statement holds after increasing \(N\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Let
    \begin{equation*} R=\mathcal A_N\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle, \qquad I=(gT_i-f_i)_i\subset R. \end{equation*}
    Since \(\mathcal A_N\) is affinoid, the ideal \(I\) is closed and \(d_1:R^m\to I\) is strict; this is the degree-zero conclusion of Lemma 5.1. In particular, there is a constant \(C\) such that every \(y\in I\) has a lift \(x\in R^m\) with \(\lVert x\rVert\leq C\lVert y\rVert\text{.}\)
    🔗
    By (7.22),
    \begin{equation*} \mathcal A\langle T_1,\ldots,T_m\rangle \cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{\,c_0}Re_\mu. \end{equation*}
    Because the relations lie in the finite head, \(d_1\) acts coefficientwise. Its image is exactly
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{\,c_0}Ie_\mu. \end{equation*}
    Indeed, the inclusion from left to right is immediate. Conversely, for a null family \((y_\mu)_\mu\) in \(I\text{,}\) choose coefficientwise lifts \(x_\mu\) with \(\lVert x_\mu\rVert\leq C\lVert y_\mu\rVert\text{;}\) then \((x_\mu)_\mu\) is again a null family. Thus the corresponding ideal over \(\mathcal A\) is closed and
    \begin{equation} \frac{\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}Re_\mu} {\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}Ie_\mu} \cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}(R/I)e_\mu\tag{7.27} \end{equation}
    as complete topological modules and as algebras. Since \(R/I=P\text{,}\) this proves (7.25).
    🔗
    Every map used in (7.27) is induced by the canonical quotient. If a further rational datum lies in the head, its first Koszul differential is again coefficientwise, so the same construction gives (7.26). Presentation independence and transitivity of rational localisation extend this compatibility to iterated refinements and to a larger finite head.
    🔗

    Corollary 7.9. Finite-head presentation.

    Every rational localisation of \(\mathcal A\) is canonically isomorphic to a ring of the form
    \begin{equation} \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{\,c_0}Pe_\mu,\tag{7.28} \end{equation}
    where \(P\) is a rational localisation of some affinoid head \(\mathcal A_N\text{.}\) Under this identification the inclusions obtained by enlarging the head have norm-nonincreasing algebra retractions, and their union is dense.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Start with a rational datum in \(\mathcal A\text{.}\) Multiplying all entries by one power of \(\varpi\) does not change the rational subset or localisation, so assume that the entries lie in \(\mathcal A_0\text{.}\) Choose an integral Bezout relation
    \begin{equation*} \varpi^\ell=\sum_j a_jd_j, \qquad a_j\in\mathcal A_0. \end{equation*}
    By (7.10), for some \(N\) the entries \(d_j\) admit approximations \(d_j'\in\mathcal A_N\cap\mathcal A_0\) satisfying \(d_j'-d_j\in\varpi^{\ell+1}\mathcal A_0\text{.}\) By Lemma 7.7, the primed datum gives the same rational subset and the same completed localisation. The primed integral Bezout relation produced in that lemma remains valid after applying the retraction \(\rho_N\text{;}\) hence the primed datum is a rational datum already in \(\mathcal A_N\text{.}\) Now apply Proposition 7.8.
    🔗
    For \(M\geq N\text{,}\) put \(P_M=(\mathcal A_M)_\alpha\text{.}\) The same proposition, with head \(M\text{,}\) identifies the localisation with the \(c_0\)-sum over the variables of index \(>M\text{.}\) Coefficient projection gives a norm-nonincreasing retraction to \(P_M\text{,}\) and finite tail truncation proves that \(\bigcup_MP_M\) is dense.

    Subsection 7.5 Sheafiness

    Theorem 7.10.

    The Huber pair \((\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\) is strongly sheafy.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    We first prove the topological sheaf condition on the rational basis of \(X=\Spa(\mathcal A,\mathcal A^\circ)\text{.}\) Let \(U\subset X\) be rational. By Corollary 7.9, choose a head presentation
    \begin{equation*} E=\mathcal O_X(U) \cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_Me_\mu, \qquad P_M=(\mathcal A_M)_\alpha, \end{equation*}
    for some \(M\text{.}\) Let \(E_U^+\) be the plus ring defining the rational subspace \(U\text{;}\) it need not equal the maximal ring \(E^\circ\text{.}\) Choose the standard closed ring of definition \(P_{M,0}\subset P_M^\circ\) furnished by the head graph presentation. The coefficientwise quotient by these relations gives a closed ring of definition
    \begin{equation*} E_0=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_{M,0}e_\mu\subseteq E_U^+. \end{equation*}
    The inclusion holds because \(E_U^+\) is an open, hence closed, subring containing the integral graph algebra. After enlarging the head, finite tail truncation shows that the corresponding head lattices are dense in \(E_0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Suppose that \(U=\bigcup_{i=1}^rU_i\) is a finite rational cover. Choose rational data for the \(U_i\) inside the Huber pair \((E,E_U^+)\text{.}\) Scale their entries into \(E_0\) and choose integral Bezout relations. After a common enlargement of the head, the density just noted and Lemma 7.7 replace all these data by data lying in one head lattice \(P_{M,0}\text{,}\) without changing any \(U_i\) or its completed section ring. Applying the coefficient projection \(E\to P_M\) to the integral Bezout relation supplied by Lemma 7.7 shows that each new datum is rational already in \(P_M\text{.}\) Let
    \begin{equation*} V_i\subset\Spa(P_M,P_M^\circ) \end{equation*}
    be the corresponding rational domains.
    🔗
    We verify that the \(V_i\) cover. The inclusion \(P_M\to E\) and coefficient projection \(E\to P_M\) are norm-nonincreasing and split each other. They therefore define morphisms of the maximal Huber pairs and a split surjection
    \begin{equation} \Spa(E,E^\circ)\longrightarrow\Spa(P_M,P_M^\circ).\tag{7.29} \end{equation}
    The maximal-pair spectrum \(\Spa(E,E^\circ)\) is a subspace of \(\Spa(E,E_U^+)=U\text{.}\) Hence the given cover restricts to a cover of \(\Spa(E,E^\circ)\text{.}\) On this subspace the restricted \(U_i\) are exactly the inverse images of the \(V_i\text{.}\) The surjectivity in (7.29) therefore implies that the \(V_i\) cover \(\Spa(P_M,P_M^\circ)\text{.}\) This avoids identifying \(U\) with the maximal-pair spectrum, which need not be correct at higher-rank points.
    🔗
    Write
    \begin{equation*} P_{M,i}=\mathcal O(V_i), \qquad P_{M,ij}=\mathcal O(V_i\cap V_j). \end{equation*}
    Naturality in Proposition 7.8 gives canonical identifications
    \begin{equation} \mathcal O_X(U_i) \cong\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_{M,i}e_\mu, \qquad \mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j) \cong\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}P_{M,ij}e_\mu,\tag{7.30} \end{equation}
    under which every restriction map is coefficientwise.
    🔗
    The affinoid algebra \(P_M\) is sheafy, so its rational-cover equalizer is an isomorphism of Banach spaces
    \begin{equation} P_M\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \operatorname{Eq}\left( \prod_iP_{M,i}\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}P_{M,ij} \right).\tag{7.31} \end{equation}
    Its inverse is bounded; fix a bound \(C\text{.}\) Let \((x_i)_i\) be a matching family in the section rings in (7.30), and write \(x_i=\sum_\mu x_{i,\mu}e_\mu\text{.}\) For each \(\mu\text{,}\) equation (7.31) glues \((x_{i,\mu})_i\) to a unique \(x_\mu\in P_M\text{,}\) with
    \begin{equation} \lVert x_\mu\rVert \leq C\max_i\lVert x_{i,\mu}\rVert.\tag{7.32} \end{equation}
    Because the cover is finite and every coefficient family is null, the right side tends to zero. Thus \(\sum_\mu x_\mu e_\mu\) is an actual element of the \(c_0\)-sum and gives the unique global section. The same bound proves that the restriction map is a topological embedding. Hence the structure presheaf satisfies the sheaf condition as complete topological rings on the rational basis.
    🔗
    For an arbitrary open cover, apply the projective-limit argument used in the last paragraph of the proof of Lemma 6.1. Namely, every rational subdomain of the open set is quasi-compact and admits a finite rational cover subordinate to the given cover; the rational-basis result glues continuously on that subdomain, and uniqueness makes these sections compatible under rational restriction. The defining projective-limit topology on arbitrary-open sections then makes the inverse gluing map continuous. Thus the public all-open structure presheaf is a sheaf of complete topological rings.
    🔗
    Finally, for auxiliary Tate variables \(V_1,\ldots,V_s\) one has, isometrically,
    \begin{equation} \mathcal A\langle V_1,\ldots,V_s\rangle \cong \widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0} \mathcal A_N\langle V_1,\ldots,V_s\rangle e_\mu.\tag{7.33} \end{equation}
    The finite heads remain affinoid, the retractions and perturbation lemma are unchanged, and the preceding proof applies verbatim. Therefore every finite Tate extension is sheafy.

    Subsection 7.6 Reducedness of all rational localisations

    Lemma 7.11.

    Let \(P\) be a reduced ring and let \(J\) be any index set. Define
    \begin{equation} \mathcal F_J(P)= \prod_{\mu\in\mathbf N^{(J)}}P\,U^\mu,\tag{7.34} \end{equation}
    with multiplication by coefficientwise finite convolution. Then \(\mathcal F_J(P)\) is reduced.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    For every finite subset \(J_0\subset J\text{,}\) setting the variables outside \(J_0\) equal to zero gives a homomorphism
    \begin{equation*} \tau_{J_0}:\mathcal F_J(P)\longrightarrow P[[U_j:j\in J_0]]. \end{equation*}
    These homomorphisms jointly detect all coefficients. A finite-variable formal power-series ring over a reduced ring is reduced: the injection \(P\to\prod_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(P)}P/\mathfrak p\) induces an injection of formal power-series rings into a product of domains. If an element of \(\mathcal F_J(P)\) is nilpotent, every \(\tau_{J_0}\) kills it; joint coefficient detection then kills the original element.
    🔗
    🔗

    Theorem 7.12.

    Every finite iterated rational localisation of \(\mathcal A\) is reduced.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By transitivity of rational localisation, it is enough to treat one rational localisation. Use Corollary 7.9 to write it as
    \begin{equation} E\cong\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\mu}^{c_0}Pe_\mu, \qquad P=(\mathcal A_N)_\alpha.\tag{7.35} \end{equation}
    The affinoid algebra \(\mathcal A_N\) is a domain by Lemma 7.2. A rational localisation of a reduced affinoid algebra is reduced [bib-BGR, Corollary 7.3.2/10]; see also [bib-KedlayaAWS, Remark 1.2.16]. Thus \(P\) is reduced.
    🔗
    The element \(W\) is a non-zero-divisor in \(\mathcal A_N\text{.}\) Affinoid rational localisation is algebraically flat [bib-BGR, Corollary 7.3.2/6]; see also [bib-ConradL15, Proposition 15.1.1, proof]. Tensoring \(0\to\mathcal A_N\xrightarrow{W}\mathcal A_N\) with \(P\) therefore shows that multiplication by \(W\) is injective on \(P\text{.}\) If \(P=0\text{,}\) the conclusion is immediate, so assume otherwise.
    🔗
    Let \(J=\{n>N\}\text{.}\) Define
    \begin{equation} \Phi:E\longrightarrow\mathcal F_J(P), \qquad \Phi\left(\sum_\mu x_\mu e_\mu\right) =\sum_\mu W^{\omega(\mu)}x_\mu U^\mu.\tag{7.36} \end{equation}
    The multiplication rule (7.23) makes \(\Phi\) multiplicative. If \(\Phi(\sum x_\mu e_\mu)=0\text{,}\) coefficient comparison gives \(W^{\omega(\mu)}x_\mu=0\) for every \(\mu\text{.}\) The \(W\)-regularity of \(P\) then gives \(x_\mu=0\) for every \(\mu\text{,}\) so \(\Phi\) is injective. By Lemma 7.11, the target is reduced; hence \(E\) is reduced.
    🔗

    Proof of Theorem 7.1.
    The uniform domain assertion is Proposition 7.3, and nonnoetherianity is Proposition 7.4. Strong sheafiness is Theorem 7.10. Reducedness of all finite iterated rational localisations is Theorem 7.12. Finally, Proposition 7.5, Proposition 7.6 identify the rational chart \(|W|\leq|\varpi|\) as a nonuniform integral domain.

    Remark 7.13. Relation with the first construction.

    The finite-jet and weighted-parity examples use different mechanisms. In the finite-jet ring, a whole tail becomes infinitely \(\varpi\)-divisible and the bad chart acquires a nilpotent. In the weighted-parity ring, every rational calculation can be moved into a noetherian finite head and then carried coefficientwise through a \(c_0\)-tail. Its bad chart remains a domain, and nonuniformity is witnessed by the unbounded family \((T_n)_{n\geq1}\) of nonnilpotent power-bounded elements. The use of an infinite monomial support and of unbounded power-bounded directions is in the tradition of the examples of Buzzard–Verberkmoes and Mihara; the finite-head coefficientwise descent is the additional feature that makes sheafiness and reducedness after iterated rational localisation accessible here.

isEmbedding : ∀ {V : Opens ↥(Spa A A⁺)} {ι : Type u}
  {U : ι → Opens ↥(Spa A A⁺)}
  (hle : ∀ i, U i ≤ V)
  (_ : (V : Set ↥(Spa A A⁺)) ⊆ ⋃ i, (U i : Set ↥(Spa A A⁺))),
  Topology.IsEmbedding (limitRestrictProd hle)
    The fields injective and glue are the usual sheaf axioms on arbitrary opens. The final field says that the resulting map to the matching-family equaliser is also a topological embedding. This is the extra condition needed for a sheaf of complete topological rings.
    🔗
    For complete Tate pairs the rational-basis and all-open predicates are equivalent:
    🔗
      theorem isSheafy_iff_isLimitSheaf :

⟨fun _ => isLimitSheaf_of_isSheafy,

The structure-presheaf equivalence identifies IsLimitSheaf A with Wedhorn’s topological-ring formulation [bib-Wedhorn, Remark 8.20]. Mathlib’s ordinary categorical sheaf predicate is weaker because it does not record the topology.

    Subsection A.3 The finite-jet example

    We finally record the definition of the example itself. In the following code, PowerSeries.Restricted R 1 is the radius-one Tate algebra in one variable over \(R\text{,}\) RestrictedLaurent K is \(K\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\text{,}\) and DualNumber R is \(R[Q]/(Q^2)\text{.}\) The source is FJP/Over/JetRings.lean.
    🔗
        abbrev L : Type u := RestrictedLaurent K

  DualNumber (PowerSeries.Restricted K (1 : ℝ))

noncomputable def jetSupport : Subring (JetC K) where
  carrier := {f |
    qCoeff K 0 f ∈ nonnegSubring K ∧
    qCoeff K 1 f ∈ nonnegSubring K}
  zero_mem' := by
    constructor <;> rw [qCoeff_zero] <;> exact zero_mem _
  one_mem' := by
    constructor <;> rw [qCoeff_one]
    · rw [if_pos rfl]; exact one_mem _
    · rw [if_neg one_ne_zero]; exact zero_mem _
  add_mem' := fun {f g} hf hg => by
    constructor <;> rw [qCoeff_add]
    · exact add_mem hf.1 hg.1
    · exact add_mem hf.2 hg.2
  neg_mem' := fun {f} hf => by
    constructor <;> rw [qCoeff_neg]
    · exact neg_mem hf.1
    · exact neg_mem hf.2
  mul_mem' := fun {f g} hf hg => by
    constructor
    · rw [qCoeff_zero_mul]
      exact mul_mem hf.1 hg.1
    · rw [qCoeff_one_mul]
      exact add_mem (mul_mem hf.1 hg.2) (mul_mem hf.2 hg.1)

abbrev JetA : Type u := ↥(jetSupport K)
    The predicate nonnegSubring K cuts out \(K\langle W\rangle\) inside \(K\langle W,W^{-1}\rangle\text{.}\) Consequently the carrier condition says that the coefficients of \(Q^0\) and \(Q^1\) have no negative powers of \(W\text{,}\) while the coefficients of \(Q^n\) for \(n\geq2\) are unrestricted. Thus JetA K is
    \begin{equation*} \left\{f_0(W)+Qf_1(W)+Q^2h(W,W^{-1},Q)\ \middle|\ \substack{f_0,f_1\in K\langle W\rangle\\ h\in L\langle Q\rangle}\right\}, \end{equation*}
    which is the support description of \(A\) used in (3.2) after inverting \(\varpi\text{.}\)

    Subsection A.4 Scope of the formalisation

    The paper’s \(A_0\) is represented by the Gauss-norm unit ball. The theorem isPowerBounded_JetA_iff identifies this unit ball with the power-bounded elements, giving \(A^\circ=A_0\text{.}\) Completeness and the Tate structure are supplied by the corresponding instances.
    🔗
    For the bad chart, chartEquiv is an exported ring equivalence. The two continuity theorems make it a homeomorphism, and the canonical-map theorem identifies \(W\) and \(Q\text{.}\) It is not bundled as a K-AlgEquiv, which is why Proposition 4.1 is stated for complete topological rings.
    🔗
    The conclusions of Lemma 5.1 are formalised in the normed setting used for the example: \(T_E\) has its Gauss norm, \(T_{E,0}\) is its unit ball, and the finite free terms have their sup norms. The statement for an arbitrary noetherian \(\varpi\)-adic \(E_0\) follows from Huber’s finite-module results and comparison of lattices, but this comparison is not separately bundled in Lean. Similarly, the development proves the finite-jet instance isSheafy_JetA of Lemma 6.1, rather than the abstract lemma for every strict Milnor square. The components of Proposition 5.3 are separate declarations.
    🔗
    The Koszul argument, the rational-chart calculation, and the transfer of sheafiness through the concrete Milnor square are contained respectively in the restricted-exactness file and the strictness and closed-image file, FJP/Over/Chart.lean, and FJP/Over/SheafTransfer.lean.

    Subsection A.5 Mathematical and code provenance

    The formal structure presheaf, rational-cover criterion, and the strongly-noetherian sheaf theorem follow Huber’s construction [bib-Huber94, Theorem 2.2 and Lemmas 2.3--2.6] and Wedhorn’s systematic formulation [bib-Wedhorn, Proposition and Definition 6.36, Remark 8.20, Definition 8.26, Theorem 8.28(b), Corollary 8.32, and Lemmas 8.33--8.34]. The passage between a fixed plus ring and all plus rings follows the standard rational-refinement argument recorded by Kedlaya [bib-KedlayaAWS, Lemma 1.6.8 and Remark 1.6.9]. The strict Koszul input is due to Ben-Bassat–Kremnizer and Bambozzi–Kremnizer; Huber’s finite-module lemmas and the bounded-denominator criterion of Buzzard–Verberkmoes supply the lattice form used in the proof.
    🔗
    The development is written in Lean 4 [bib-Lean4] on top of mathlib [bib-mathlib]. The formalisation-specific files live in AINTLIB [bib-AINTLIB]. Its restricted power-series and restricted Laurent-series infrastructure includes Apache-2.0-licensed code adapted from William Coram’s PhD repository, with equivalence code attributed there to Bingyu Xia [bib-CoramXia]. The vendored source headers and commit history retain those attributions. The finite-jet definitions, localisation proofs, and sheaf-transfer endpoints discussed in this appendix are the AINTLIB-specific part of the development.
